\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusions}

MouseClaimBench is an evidence-constrained \ac{kbs} for computational mouse-brain claims. It externalizes a versioned knowledge profile, preserves source-scale facts and provenance, applies prioritized non-compensatory rules, and returns a machine-readable proof trace. Prediction, reproducibility, topology, direction, structure--function association, mechanism, causality, and digital-twin scope remain distinct targets.

Exhaustive state tests establish deterministic rule behaviour for the mechanistic claim. In the known-truth benchmark, the policy reduces \ac{fpr} from $0.271$ for a prediction shortcut to $0.019$, with \ac{fnr} increasing from $0.007$ to $0.070$. Four real cases produce 40 bounded decisions that are all reproduced with complete traces. External controls further show why retrieval cannot replace support inference and association cannot replace causal direction.

These results do not establish biological truth or universal scientific validity. Only the mouse-brain profile is evaluated, the \ac{microns} endpoint is internal to one volume, and no case supports external replication, causal whole-brain identification, or a complete digital twin. Future work should freeze the profile before evaluation on independently selected studies, test structure--function evidence across animals with network-aware inference, integrate stronger provenance-bearing evidence producers, and extend semantic interoperability. Another domain profile is justified only when its vocabulary and validation cases are independently available.

The conclusion is deliberately narrow. Accurate prediction and reproducible execution do not authorize mechanistic, causal, or digital-twin wording by themselves. MouseClaimBench represents this boundary as inspectable knowledge and records why each supported or restricted conclusion was reached.
