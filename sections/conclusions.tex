\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This paper introduced ClaimBench, an executable DSS for matching scientific AI wording to heterogeneous computational evidence. Its central mechanism is a claim contract with mandatory evidence blocks. The resulting gate is non-compensatory, exposes uncertainty, binds decisions to artifacts, and keeps LLM-assisted claim discovery outside scientific authorization.

The evaluation establishes that this mechanism is operational under the declared conditions. Across 1,152 known-truth claim opportunities, the full gate produces no false authorization or false rejection. Correlation-only, leaderboard-only, reliability-only, and compensatory alternatives reintroduce measurable overclaiming or excessive conservativeness. The threshold grid identifies 108 safe and 135 dangerous configurations. SciFact and Tuebingen demonstrate that retrieval and correlation can remain unsafe bases for support and causal direction. The frozen package reproduces all 15 stages and passes its unified and threat-model checks.

These findings support a bounded contribution. ClaimBench makes the evidence-to-claim decision explicit, traceable, and reproducible. It does not establish universal claim validity, automated peer review, state-of-the-art fact checking, or causal-discovery performance. Its synthetic labels encode operational semantics, its external controls diagnose selected failure modes, and its current claim extractor lacks an expert-annotated evaluation corpus.

The next decisive validation is human and external. A blinded multi-domain manuscript study must determine whether the framework reduces false authorization, improves reviewer agreement, and produces better calibrated revisions without unacceptable workload. Until that evidence exists, ClaimBench should be used as a transparent pre-submission and review-support instrument. Its broader message is nevertheless clear: a reproducible score is not the end of scientific AI evaluation. Every strong interpretation should carry an explicit account of the evidence that authorizes it and the conditions that would block it.
