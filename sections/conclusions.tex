\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This paper presented ClaimBench, an executable decision-support framework for governing scientific AI claims. The framework separates evidence dimensions that are often conflated in applied AI papers and applies a non-compensatory gate that prevents prediction, reproducibility, or retrieval quality from authorizing unsupported mechanistic, causal, digital-twin, or state-of-the-art wording.

The current package passes 15 of 15 reproduction stages, 10 of 10 unified report criteria, and 7 of 7 threat-model checks. Synthetic known-truth experiments validate the operational semantics of the gate. SciFact and Tuebingen provide external controls for claim-evidence and causal-overclaiming risks. Component ablations show that removing safeguards reintroduces identifiable failure modes. The LLM-ready layer supports candidate extraction while preserving a strict rule: executable artifacts, not LLM outputs, authorize claims.

The contribution is intentionally bounded. ClaimBench is not a new predictor, a causal-discovery method, or an automated reviewer. Its value is to make the evidence-to-claim decision explicit before publication. The broader result is that scientific AI evaluation should not stop at reproducible scores. It should also state which interpretations those scores can and cannot support. Future work should evaluate multiple LLM extractors, extend the claim taxonomy to additional high-stakes AI domains, and test whether ClaimBench artifacts improve reviewer agreement and revision efficiency.
