\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evaluation addresses four questions. First, does the executable system implement the declared authorization semantics and return exact deficits? Second, does the package-integrity layer block controlled attacks that structural validation or hash checking alone cannot block? Third, does the implementation remain usable as the number of evidence packages and artifacts grows? Fourth, can frozen protocols produce bounded positive and negative decisions on external mouse-brain resources without changing the contract after observing numerical outcomes? Table~\ref{tab:experiment-design-v2} defines the evidence unit and maximum interpretation for each question.

\subsection{Experimental design and decision rules}
\label{subsec:experimental-design}

The contract benchmark generates five case families from every profile claim. They contain pristine complete packages, one changed status, one omitted mandatory block, one omitted required observation, and all pairwise combinations of the four non-passing states. The resulting 5,497 cases have an exact expected authorization and deficit set defined from Eqs.~\ref{eq:admissibility}--\ref{eq:core-authorization}. The primary Python engine is compared with a separately implemented \ac{asp} program on 262 deterministically selected cases. The same 5,497 cases are serialized to \ac{rdf} and evaluated by \texttt{pySHACL} 0.40.1. Endpoints are zero false authorizations, zero false rejections, exact deficit-set recovery, and exact cross-engine equivalence. JSON-LD round-trip equivalence is evaluated through graph isomorphism.

Formal verification uses 1,000 deterministic random evidence packages for each of the 10 claims. Every package is evaluated for soundness, completeness, exact deficits, input-order invariance, irrelevant-evidence invariance, and monotonic degradation whenever a passed block exists. Outside-profile closure is evaluated separately. Property-based tests generate additional status and observation mutations during the software suite. The reported 55,031 checks refer to the frozen deterministic experiment, not to an exhaustive proof over arbitrary future profiles.

The integrity benchmark applies eight attacks defined in Section~\ref{subsec:standards-integrity}. For each of the 10 claims, it evaluates one pristine package, every single attack, and all 28 pairwise attack combinations. This produces 370 packages, of which 360 are attacked. A false authorization occurs when an attacked package is authorized:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{FAR}_{\mathrm{attack}}=
\frac{\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{1}[m\text{ attacked}]A^{*}_{\Pi}(c_m,B_m,m)}
{\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{1}[m\text{ attacked}]}.
\label{eq:attack-far}
\end{equation}
We compare the complete integrity gate with the core profile alone, a hash-only baseline, and eight leave-one-control-out systems. Exact trace recovery requires the detected deficit codes to equal the applied attack set. The benchmark estimates behaviour only for these deterministic attacks. It is not a security evaluation against an adaptive adversary.

Scalability is measured on one Apple arm64 host with Python 3.14.4. Pristine authorization batches contain 100, 1,000, and 10,000 packages drawn cyclically from all profile claims. Each batch is repeated five times after 100 warm-up decisions. A separate experiment expands one valid digital-twin package to 25, 100, 1,000, and 5,000 uniquely content-addressed artifacts. Median wall-clock time and all repetitions are stored. These measurements characterize one implementation and host. No portable latency guarantee is inferred.

Three artifact studies complement the controlled benchmarks. The first reuses five result packages assembled during the preceding MouseBrainBench study and asks a different question: whether profile v2 authorizes their bounded target claim and strict digital-twin claim. These mappings are retrospective and are not counted as prospective validation. The \ac{microns} endpoint was fixed after discovery and evaluated in two non-overlapping hold-out windows within one tissue volume. Directed pair regression controls log soma distance, its square, pre- and postsynaptic degree, and coarse cell type. Dependence is addressed with directed dyadic cluster covariance and 1,000 simultaneous node-label Freedman--Lane permutations. These hold-outs provide internal reproduction, not independent biological replication.

Two external \ac{dandi} protocols were committed before numerical asset access. DANDI:001176 was selected from metadata describing 29 subjects and simultaneous cholinergic axon, cortical \ac{ach}, pupil, and treadmill signals \citep{neyhart2024ach}. The protocol required at least 20 usable subjects and prohibited threshold or endpoint repair. After catalog inspection, 28 schema-valid sessions came from only 5 subjects. The predictive model was therefore not executed.

DANDI:000039 contains contrast-tuning recordings associated with a published visual-cortex study \citep{millman2020vip}. Before inspecting its asset catalog or values, the protocol fixed one lexicographically first \texttt{behavior+ophys.nwb} asset per subject, a trial-level population mean event response, five contrast and direction regressors, Ridge regression with $\alpha=1$, and an intercept comparator. The first 60\% of trials train the model, the next 20\% remain reserved, and the final 20\% form the test partition. Authorization requires at least 25 subjects, median held-out correlation at least 0.10, a positive 95\% mouse-bootstrap lower bound, at least 65\% positive subjects, and pooled squared error below the intercept. All conditions must pass. The bootstrap resamples subjects 5,000 times and therefore targets population stability across mice rather than trial-level independence.

\subsection{Conformance and formal verification results}
\label{subsec:conformance-results}

Table~\ref{tab:standards-formal-results} reports the principal conformance results. The Python engine produces no false authorization or false rejection in 5,497 cases and recovers every expected deficit set. The independent \ac{asp} implementation matches status and deficits in all 262 selected cases. The \ac{shacl} engine also classifies all 5,497 cases correctly, returns every expected structural deficit, and agrees exactly with Python. The exported profile contains 318 \ac{rdf} triples representing all 10 claims and 22 evidence blocks. JSON-LD serialization and parsing preserve graph isomorphism.

\input{tables/standards_formal_results}

These results separate two conclusions. First, the profile semantics are implemented consistently across three execution technologies. Second, standard \ac{shacl} validation reproduces the structural contract. Structural completeness and deficit reporting are therefore not unique capabilities of MouseClaimBench. The additional contribution must be assessed through domain requirements, package integrity, controlled attacks, and real applications.

The 10,000 deterministic random packages produce 55,031 property checks with no violation. Soundness, completeness, deficit identity, input-order invariance, and irrelevant-evidence invariance each pass 10,000 checks. Monotonic degradation passes all 5,030 applicable checks. Outside-profile closure also passes. These results establish the operational semantics for profile v2. They do not prove that its evidence requirements are scientifically complete.

\subsection{Integrity attacks, ablation, and scalability}
\label{subsec:integrity-results}

The complete gate detects the exact attack set in all 370 packages. It produces no false rejection on the 10 pristine packages and no false authorization on the 360 attacked packages. The core profile does not inspect package integrity and therefore authorizes all 360 attacked packages. Hash checking alone blocks content tampering but authorizes 280 packages affected by profile substitution, broken or cyclic lineage, false independence, cohort overlap, contradictory attestations, or missing block provenance.

\input{figures/integrity_ablation_v2}

The leave-one-out result is uniform and informative. Removing any one of the eight controls creates 10 false authorizations, corresponding to the single-attack package for that omitted control in each claim. Pairwise attacks containing another enabled control remain blocked. Table~\ref{tab:integrity-ablation-v2} gives the exact values, while Fig.~\ref{fig:integrity-ablation-v2} shows the contrast between incomplete baselines and the full system. The result demonstrates necessity under the declared attack construction. It does not imply that the eight controls are sufficient for every real provenance threat.

Batch throughput remains between 63,504 and 65,465 authorizations per second. Median time is 0.00153 s for 100 packages, 0.01575 s for 1,000, and 0.15419 s for 10,000. A valid package with 5,000 artifacts is evaluated in a median of 0.00255 s. The descriptive log--log slope between artifact count and latency is 0.754 over the four measured points. This value is not presented as an asymptotic complexity proof because fixed overhead dominates the smallest packages. All pristine decisions remain authorized at every scale.

\subsection{Mouse-brain artifacts and prospective external applications}
\label{subsec:application-results}

The retrospective profile-v2 mapping contains five target claims and the corresponding five strict digital-twin claims. Three bounded target claims are authorized. Static Sensorium authorizes prediction for its declared five-dataset comparison. The \ac{ibl} case authorizes topology-specific behavioural prediction under its locked comparison. \ac{microns} authorizes one local observational structure--function association. Allen directed topology remains unauthorized because prediction, topology, direction, uncertainty, robustness, and data-quality requirements do not all pass. Dynamic Sensorium remains unauthorized because the stored response-reliability quality block requires review. None of the five cases authorizes the strict digital-twin claim.

The dependence-aware \ac{microns} analysis changes the status of the local association without changing its scope. The connected-pair coefficient is 0.0106 in discovery, 0.0178 in hold-out 1, and 0.0117 in hold-out 2. Directed dyadic standard errors are 0.00200, 0.00209, and 0.00205. Two-sided dyadic $p$-values are $1.39\times10^{-7}$, $5.78\times10^{-17}$, and $1.76\times10^{-8}$. The simultaneous node-label permutation $p$-value is 0.000999 in every window. Shared-neuron dependence is therefore addressed more conservatively than naive pair-level inference. All windows nevertheless come from one \ac{microns} volume, and readout-location similarity is a derived functional descriptor rather than a causal measurement.

The two prospective DANDI outcomes are deliberately asymmetric. DANDI:001176 fails the frozen availability condition with 5 usable subjects against a minimum of 20. No predictive fit is reported, and the endpoint is not weakened after seeing the catalog. DANDI:000039 yields 32 selected and usable mice. Its median held-out correlation is 0.310, the mouse-bootstrap 95\% interval is $[0.207,0.369]$, and 93.75\% of subjects have positive correlation. Pooled model squared error is 11.579 compared with 12.244 for the training-intercept baseline. All five frozen acceptance conditions pass, so the system authorizes only bounded population-response prediction within this resource.

\input{figures/prospective_applications_v2}

The positive result is not a state-of-the-art model claim. Ridge regression was chosen because the experiment tests prospective authorization rather than neural architecture. The negative result is equally part of the method evaluation. Together they show that a frozen knowledge contract can preserve both authorization and refusal without post-outcome threshold repair. The final release verifies all source revisions, profile and protocol hashes, exact public DANDI asset identifiers, and the absence of \texttt{-dirty} artifacts.
