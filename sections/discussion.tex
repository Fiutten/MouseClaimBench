\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The evaluation supports MouseClaimBench as a bounded \ac{kbs} for computational mouse-brain claims. The contribution is an explicit representation of required evidence and a deterministic connection between domain knowledge and source artifacts. Prediction, reproducibility, topology, direction, structure--function association, intervention, and anatomical scope remain separate because they answer different scientific questions.

\subsection{Interpretation and applicability}
\label{subsec:kbs-lessons}

The separation of profile and facts makes assumptions inspectable. The profile requires prediction, internal reproduction, topology, and direction for mechanism. Allen supplies facts for these blocks without case-specific inference code. Reproduction passes, while topology and direction fail, so mechanism is blocked. Non-compensation is decisive. The prediction shortcut has low \ac{fnr} but creates 785 false authorizations. Equation~\ref{eq:five-state-gate} prevents this substitution while retaining ten supported real-case claims.

Proof traces also require two interpretations. Exact decisions, witnesses, and profile hashes establish faithful computation. They do not establish that the taxonomy is unique or every predicate biologically sufficient. Likewise, negative decisions are informative outputs. They identify the failed, absent, or untargeted requirement rather than hiding it in a global score.

The engine can load another profile, but this is architectural extensibility rather than empirical generality. A new domain requires an independently specified vocabulary, predicates, priorities, and cases. Optional \ac{llm} extraction follows the same boundary. It may propose a candidate but cannot create evidence or authorize it. Relative to knowledge graphs, claim verifiers, and assurance cases, MouseClaimBench performs the narrower task of provenance-bound claim authorization and can feed, but not replace, broader assurance workflows \citep{borrego2025research,wadden2020scifact,batarseh2021assurance}.

\subsection{Limitations and validity threats}
\label{subsec:limitations}

Construct validity depends on the proposed mouse-brain profile. Its requirements and thresholds are explicit, but other researchers may dispute them. The oracle reduces circularity because graphs and interventions are fixed independently of evidence thresholds, yet its five low-dimensional regimes do not cover arbitrary dynamics, noise, or sample sizes.

The four real cases are complementary audits rather than a representative sample. Frozen-decision agreement demonstrates migration fidelity, not external biological validity. The \ac{microns} endpoint is confined to one cortical volume. Its hold-outs provide internal reproduction rather than replication across animals. Unit-cluster bootstrap addresses neuron reuse, but network-dependent variance estimators and externally fixed endpoints remain necessary.

SciFact and Tuebingen are substitution controls rather than competitive evaluations. The proof trace has not been tested for human comprehensibility. This paper does not claim reduced review time, improved agreement, or better decision quality. The exported graph is operational rather than a complete OWL or PROV-O representation. Future interoperability must preserve immutable profile identity and evidence provenance.
