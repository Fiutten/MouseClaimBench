\subsection{Scientific claims, knowledge systems, and assurance}
\label{subsec:claims-assurance}

Scientific claim verification commonly decomposes the task into retrieval, evidence selection, and support or contradiction classification \citep{vladika2023survey}. SciFact introduced expert-written claims grounded in scientific abstracts, while MultiVerS and SciFact-Open extended full-document and open-domain reasoning \citep{wadden2020scifact,wadden2022multivers,wadden2022scifactopen}. SciTab, SciVer, SciTrue, PhyVer, and later multimodal approaches include tables, figures, physical measurements, and richer evidence paths \citep{lu2023scitab,wang2025sciver,tan2026scitrue,chen2026phyver,zhang2026mever}. CLAIM-BENCH evaluates the extraction and linkage of claim--evidence pairs by language models \citep{javaji2025claimbench}. These systems address documentary support. MouseClaimBench instead assumes that evidence artifacts and their source predicates have been produced, then evaluates whether their scientific type and provenance satisfy a declared authorization contract. A retrieved sentence cannot substitute for an intervention, an independent cohort, or a topology control.

Rule-based expert systems separate domain knowledge, facts, inference, and explanation \citep{buchanan1984rulebased,studer1998knowledge}. Selective prediction adds a learned reject option when a prediction should not be issued \citep{geifman2019selectivenet}. MouseClaimBench instead returns all typed contract deficits and does not learn an abstention threshold. Classical validation distinguishes structural correctness from the validity of acquired domain knowledge \citep{benbasat1989validation,knauf2002validation}. This distinction remains central here. Exhaustive tests and multiple solvers can establish that an implementation follows its profile, but they cannot establish that the profile is a complete or universally accepted account of neuroscience.

Assurance cases organize claims, arguments, assumptions, defeaters, and evidence for a defined context of use \citep{batarseh2021assurance,mohamad2021assurance}. Current work also studies assurance cases as attributed graphs and identifies provenance-related structural differences between human- and machine-generated arguments \citep{ikhwantri2026assurancegraphs}. Compliance-by-construction argument graphs combine typed arguments, deterministic admissibility constraints, and PROV-aligned ledgers for AI-assisted certification records \citep{moghaddam2026compliance}. Claim-level auditability has similarly been proposed for research agents through persistent provenance and protocolized validation \citep{rasheed2026auditability}. MouseClaimBench does not use a generative model to draft an assurance case. It operationalizes a domain claim profile, tests complete deficits across independent execution paths, measures false authorization under controlled package attacks, and evaluates frozen protocols on external scientific artifacts.

\subsection{Semantic validation, provenance, and reproducibility}
\label{subsec:semantic-standards}

\ac{shacl} validates an \ac{rdf} data graph against a shapes graph and returns a machine-readable validation report \citep{w3c2017shacl}. \ac{prov} represents entities, activities, agents, and derivation relations for interoperable provenance \citep{lebo2013provo}. MouseClaimBench reuses both standards. Profile definitions and evidence packages are exported as \ac{rdf}/JSON-LD, and an external \ac{shacl} engine checks field and relation constraints. Consequently, structural graph validation and provenance interchange are not presented as novel results.

The remaining problem is semantic and relational. A syntactically valid graph may cite the wrong profile, reuse the same artifact as independent evidence, declare overlapping cohorts as disjoint, contain incompatible attestations, or authorize a claim whose mandatory scientific block failed. These conditions cross the boundary between graph shape, package integrity, and domain policy. MouseClaimBench handles them through an additional non-compensatory integrity gate and returns the complete witnesses for every detected deficit.

Reproducibility guidance, \ac{fair} principles, model cards, datasheets, and research-software standards improve disclosure and reuse \citep{nasem2019reproducibility,wilkinson2016fair,mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets,pineau2021reproducibility,barker2022fair4rs}. \ac{nwb} provides a common neurophysiology data language and underlies reuse through \ac{dandi} \citep{rubel2022nwb}. MouseClaimBench records exact resource versions, asset identifiers, official hashes, code revisions, protocol hashes, and bounded interpretations. It also distinguishes clean computational reproduction, within-resource reproduction, and external study replication. These terms cannot be promoted into one another.

Model \ac{vv} and uncertainty-quantification frameworks assess implementation correctness, empirical adequacy, uncertainty, credibility, and context of use \citep{oberkampf2010verification,sargent2013verification,asme2018vv40,riedmaier2021vvuq}. MouseClaimBench does not replace those analyses. It consumes their resulting artifacts as typed evidence and prevents a passed validation dimension from authorizing another claim whose required evidence is absent.

\subsection{Validation boundaries in computational mouse-brain research}
\label{subsec:mouse-boundaries}

Mouse-brain resources differ in anatomical scale and experimental purpose. Allen tracer data and derived connectomes support mesoscale structural analysis \citep{oh2014mesoscale,knox2019connectome}. Sensorium and Dynamic Sensorium evaluate neural response prediction under static and video stimuli \citep{willeke2022sensorium,turishcheva2023dynamic,turishcheva2024retrospective}. Recent work extends predictive generalization and analyzes population-response geometry in visual-cortex digital twins, but prediction remains distinct from unique mechanistic identification \citep{wang2025foundation,liscai2025population}. \ac{microns} provides detailed anatomy and function in visual cortex and supports local structure--function analysis \citep{bae2025microns,ding2025wiring}. It is not a measured synaptic connectome of the complete mouse brain.

The Virtual Mouse Brain, \ac{bmtk}, and detailed \ac{vone} models solve simulation and network-construction problems at different scales \citep{melozzi2017virtualmouse,dai2020bmtk,billeh2020v1}. Observational causal-direction benchmarks show that direction requires assumptions beyond association \citep{mooij2016cause}. Digital Twin Brain and MouseDTB proposals further motivate explicit distinctions among signal prediction, topology, direction, intervention, entity specificity, anatomical coverage, and operational updating \citep{xiong2023dtb,chen2026mousedtb}. MouseClaimBench does not compete with these simulators. It evaluates which bounded statements their associated evidence packages may authorize.

The scoped literature review did not identify a system combining domain-specific evidence predicates, profile-relative non-compensation, complete multi-deficit traces, independent \ac{asp} and \ac{shacl} conformance, package-dependence attacks, prospective external protocols, and mouse-brain artifact applications. This observation supports differentiation, not global priority. Each component has substantial prior art, and the contribution must be judged on the integration, executable evaluation, and scientific boundaries reported here.
