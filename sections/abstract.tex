Computational mouse-brain studies combine evidence with distinct meanings. Reproducible execution or accurate prediction may support a bounded result while leaving mechanism, causality, or digital-twin scope unauthorized. MouseClaimBench is an integrity-aware knowledge-based system whose authorization contracts bind domain evidence semantics, structural admissibility, provenance integrity, and complete non-compensatory deficits. It uses \ac{rdf}/\ac{prov} for artifact derivations, \ac{shacl} for evidence schemas, and relational checks for package integrity. Python and \ac{shacl} agree on all 5,497 structural contract cases, while an independently implemented \ac{asp} path agrees on a deterministically selected subset of 262 cases. Six formalized properties and outside-profile closure show no violation in 55,031 deterministic property checks. Under the declared deterministic threat model, the complete gate produces no false authorization across 360 attacked packages. A profile-only system authorizes all 360, a hash-only baseline authorizes 280, and removing any one of eight controls reintroduces false authorization. Two pre-access frozen \ac{dandi} protocols yield one availability refusal, with 5 usable subjects against 20 required, and one bounded predictive authorization across 32 mice, with median held-out correlation 0.310 and bootstrap lower bound 0.207. A dependence-aware \ac{microns} application authorizes one local observational structure--function association, but no causal or whole-brain claim. MouseClaimBench is an executable scientific-authorization layer, not a biological truth engine or a complete digital mouse brain.
