Artificial Intelligence (AI) in scientific research increasingly produces models whose outputs are presented as predictive, reproducible, mechanistic, causal, or digital-twin evidence. Conventional evaluation reports scores and artifacts but rarely determines which interpretation those results authorize. This paper presents ClaimBench, an executable Decision Support System for evidence-aware governance of scientific AI claims. A claim is encoded as a contract that links its permitted wording to mandatory evidence blocks. A non-compensatory gate prevents a strong result in one dimension from replacing absent evidence in another, while an explicit uncertain state preserves threshold-sensitive cases. ClaimBench combines known-truth stress tests, sensitivity analysis, external controls using SciFact and the Tuebingen Cause-Effect Pairs, manuscript auditing, component ablation, threat modelling, and clean-release verification. A bounded Large Language Model interface may propose candidate claims, but only executable artifacts can authorize them. In 144 synthetic cases, the full gate produces no false claim authorizations, whereas correlation-only and compensatory alternatives yield Overclaiming Risk Index values of 0.521 and 0.135. The threshold study identifies 108 safe and 135 dangerous configurations. External controls expose 35 lexical false supports in SciFact and 79 correlation-only causal-direction overclaims in Tuebingen. The frozen package passes 15 of 15 reproduction stages, 10 of 10 unified criteria, and 7 of 7 threat-model checks. These results support ClaimBench as a traceable decision aid for matching scientific wording to available evidence. They do not establish automated peer review, causal discovery performance, or universal claim validity.
