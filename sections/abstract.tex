Computational mouse-brain studies combine predictive, anatomical, connectomic, and simulation evidence whose scientific meanings are not interchangeable. This paper presents MouseClaimBench, an evidence-constrained knowledge-based system that authorizes claims under an explicit operational policy. Its versioned, author-proposed profile represents ten claim types, twenty-two claim-to-evidence relations, and five prioritized non-compensatory rules. A machine-readable curation record states the rationale, scope, exceptions, and sources for every relation. Evidence facts retain original observations, source predicates, and revisions, while each disposition includes its decisive rule and witnesses. Evaluation combines exhaustive rule tests, an oracle structural-equation benchmark, four artifact-grounded mouse-brain cases, and external substitution controls. Across 500 generated cases and 5,000 dependent claim decisions per policy, MouseClaimBench obtains a false-positive rate of 0.019 (95\% case-cluster \ac{ci} 0.014--0.025) and a false-negative rate of 0.070 (0.056--0.084). An equal-weight compensatory policy obtains 0.050 and 0.041, while a prediction shortcut obtains 0.271 and 0.007. The engine also reproduces forty author-generated migration decisions with complete traces. The real cases support bounded prediction, within-resource reproduction, topology, and local observational structure--function statements, but no causal, externally replicated, whole-brain, or complete entity-specific digital-twin claim. MouseClaimBench therefore contributes an auditable reasoning and benchmarking layer for scientific claim authorization. The profile's logical consistency and operational behaviour are evaluated, but independent expert consensus and prospective external validity are not claimed.
