Computational mouse-brain studies combine evidence with distinct scientific meanings. Reproducibility or accurate prediction may support a bounded result while leaving mechanism, causality, or digital-twin scope unauthorized. This paper presents MouseClaimBench, a standards-aware knowledge-based system that evaluates evidence packages against a versioned non-compensatory claim profile. The method represents source artifacts and derivations in \ac{rdf}/\ac{prov}, validates evidence schemas with \ac{shacl}, checks hashes, lineage, cohort independence, and attestations, and returns every unmet requirement instead of a compensatory score. Three independent execution paths agree on all 5,497 contract cases. Six formal properties hold in 55,031 deterministic checks. In a controlled threat model, the complete integrity gate produces no false authorization across 360 attacked packages, while a profile-only system authorizes all 360 and a hash-only baseline authorizes 280. Removing any one of eight integrity controls reintroduces false authorization. Two protocols were frozen before numerical access to external \ac{dandi} resources. The first remains negative because only 5 subjects satisfy a predeclared minimum of 20. The second uses 32 mice and authorizes only held-out population-response prediction, with a median subject correlation of 0.310 and a subject-bootstrap lower bound of 0.207. A dependence-aware \ac{microns} application authorizes one local observational structure--function association but no causal or whole-brain statement. MouseClaimBench contributes an executable authorization layer for scientific evidence, not a biological truth engine or a complete digital mouse brain.
