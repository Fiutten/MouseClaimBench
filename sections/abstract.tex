Computational mouse-brain studies combine predictive, anatomical, connectomic, and simulation evidence that does not support the same scientific interpretations. This paper presents MouseClaimBench, an executable decision support system that maps candidate wording to claim-specific evidence contracts. The method preserves observations in their original domain scales, separates computational reproducibility, internal reproduction, and external replication, and applies a non-compensatory gate with five workflow dispositions. Evaluation combines an oracle structural-equation benchmark, four artifact-grounded mouse-brain cases, SciFact and Tuebingen controls, legacy software-conformance tests, and a clean release audit. Across 500 generated cases and 5,000 claim decisions per policy, the contract obtains a false-positive rate of 0.019 (95\% \ac{ci} 0.015--0.025) and a false-negative rate of 0.070 (0.059--0.081). A prediction-only shortcut obtains 0.271 and 0.007, respectively. The real cases authorize only bounded statements: internal reproduction for Allen Visual Behavior Neuropixels, predictive and topographic evidence for Static Sensorium, internally reproduced predictive evidence for Dynamic Sensorium, and a local observational structure--function association for Machine Intelligence from Cortical Networks. No real case authorizes causality, external biological replication, or a complete digital twin. A SciFact baseline calibrated on 809 training claims reduces development false authorization to 0.068 but raises false rejection to 0.718, exposing the cost of conservative decisions. MouseClaimBench therefore contributes a traceable policy and implementation for scientific claim governance. Human decision benefit remains untested, and an expert protocol is provided without presenting it as completed evidence.
