Computational mouse-brain studies combine predictive, anatomical, connectomic, and simulation evidence whose scientific meanings are not interchangeable. This paper presents MouseClaimBench, an evidence-constrained knowledge-based system that determines whether a declared evidence package supports a specific mouse-brain claim. Its versioned knowledge profile represents ten claim types, their required evidence blocks, and five prioritized non-compensatory rules. Evidence facts retain original-scale observations, source predicates, rationales, and revisions. Each conclusion includes the fired rule, its witnesses, and the profile hash. Evaluation combines an oracle structural-equation benchmark, four artifact-grounded mouse-brain cases, SciFact and Tuebingen controls, and exhaustive rule tests. Across 500 generated cases and 5,000 claim decisions per policy, MouseClaimBench obtains a false-positive rate of 0.019 (95\% \ac{ci} 0.015--0.025) and a false-negative rate of 0.070 (0.059--0.081). A prediction-only shortcut obtains 0.271 and 0.007. The knowledge engine exactly reproduces all 40 frozen decisions for Allen Visual Behavior Neuropixels, Static Sensorium, Dynamic Sensorium, and Machine Intelligence from Cortical Networks, while producing a complete inference trace for every decision. These cases support bounded prediction, internal reproduction, topology, and local structure--function statements, but no causal, externally replicated, whole-brain, or complete digital-twin claim. MouseClaimBench therefore contributes a reproducible knowledge representation and reasoning layer for scientific claim validation. Only the mouse-brain profile is empirically evaluated, and conformance to its policy is not presented as biological truth.
