\ac{ai} increasingly supports neurocomputational modelling, where predictive, reproducible, mechanistic, causal, and digital-twin interpretations are often attached to heterogeneous evidence. Mouse-brain modelling is a demanding case because anatomical, electrophysiological, connectomic, predictive, and simulation resources describe different scales. Conventional evaluation reports scores and artifacts but rarely determines which interpretations they authorize. This paper presents ClaimBench, an executable \ac{dss} that links permitted wording to mandatory evidence blocks. A non-compensatory gate prevents one strong result from replacing absent evidence elsewhere, while an uncertain state preserves threshold-sensitive decisions. ClaimBench is instantiated on a frozen mouse-brain modelling corpus and evaluated with known-truth stress tests, sensitivity analysis, SciFact and Tuebingen external controls, component ablation, threat modelling, and clean-release verification. A bounded \ac{llm} interface may propose claims, but only executable artifacts authorize them. Across 144 synthetic cases, the full gate produces no false authorizations, whereas correlation-only and compensatory alternatives yield \ac{ori} values of 0.521 and 0.135. The threshold study identifies 108 safe and 135 dangerous configurations. External controls expose 35 lexical false supports in SciFact and 79 correlation-only causal-direction overclaims in Tuebingen. The frozen package passes 15 of 15 reproduction stages, 10 of 10 unified criteria, and 7 of 7 threat-model checks. ClaimBench therefore provides traceable decision support for matching mouse-brain modelling claims to available evidence. It does not establish biological truth, automated peer review, causal-discovery performance, or universal claim validity.
