\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

MouseClaimBench connects four areas that usually operate on different objects. Knowledge engineering represents domain concepts and inference procedures. Scientific claim verification links statements to documentary evidence. Assurance and model credibility connect evidence to a context of use. Neurocomputational modelling produces predictive, structural, and dynamical representations. The proposed system joins these levels by mapping provenance-bearing domain facts to explicit claim semantics.

\subsection{Knowledge representation and scientific evidence}
\label{subsec:knowledge-engineering}

Rule-based expert systems separate a knowledge base from an inference engine, while later knowledge-engineering methods make domain assumptions and problem-solving procedures explicit \citep{buchanan1984rulebased,studer1998knowledge}. Ontologies formalize concepts and relations, and W3C PROV represents derivation and provenance \citep{guarino1998formal,lebo2013provo}. MouseClaimBench uses a bounded operational ontology rather than a general neuroscience ontology. Claim types, evidence blocks, states, and prioritized rules reside in a versioned profile. Study observations instantiate evidence facts with their predicates and sources.

Knowledge acquisition and system verification are not equivalent to domain validation. Lifecycle approaches require evidence for the acquired knowledge as well as tests of implementation behaviour, while rule-system validation commonly adds expert comparison and independently constructed test cases \citep{benbasat1989validation,knauf2002validation}. MouseClaimBench implements relation-level curation records, structural verification, known-truth tests, and artifact conformance. It does not report expert-panel agreement or consensus validity. This distinction limits the contribution but also makes its present validation stage explicit.

Recent systems combine ontologies, graphs, and learned representations to infer scientific or biomedical relations. ResearchLink ranks candidate research hypotheses, while ontology-enriched causal graphs combine semantic knowledge and learned relations \citep{borrego2025research,pham2026causal}. MouseClaimBench addresses a narrower decision. It does not generate hypotheses or learn missing links. It determines whether observed evidence satisfies the declared requirements of an existing claim, including negative and scope-limited conclusions.

\subsection{Claim verification, assurance, and reproducibility}
\label{subsec:claims-assurance}

Scientific claim verification commonly combines retrieval, rationale selection, and support classification. SciFact, MultiVerS, and SciFact-Open expose evidence retrieval, full-document reasoning, and open-domain limitations \citep{wadden2020scifact,wadden2022multivers,wadden2022scifactopen}. Later benchmarks incorporate tables, figures, source traceability, and executable physical evidence \citep{vladika2023survey,lu2023scitab,wang2025sciver,tan2026scitrue,chen2026phyver,zhang2026mever}. These methods ask whether documentary content supports a proposition. MouseClaimBench asks whether the available evidence is of the correct scientific type for that proposition. Retrieval success cannot create an intervention or external replication.

Selective prediction provides abstention, and language models can assist evidence discovery \citep{geifman2019selectivenet,fadeeva2024factchecking}. One reject state is insufficient here because an executed failure, missing observation, untargeted protocol, and expert judgement imply different next actions. MouseClaimBench preserves these states. Candidate extraction is outside the evaluated authorization method.

Model \ac{vv}, \ac{uq}, and assurance cases connect evidence to a declared use through claims, assumptions, and defeaters \citep{oberkampf2010verification,sargent2013verification,riedmaier2021vvuq,asme2018vv40,batarseh2021assurance,mohamad2021assurance}. Reproducibility guidance, model cards, datasheets, and \ac{fair} principles improve traceability and disclosure \citep{nasem2019reproducibility,wilkinson2016fair,mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets,pineau2021reproducibility,barker2022fair4rs}. MouseClaimBench follows the distinction between computational reproducibility with the same data and methods and replication with new data, while retaining within-resource reproduction as an explicitly weaker intermediate category. The full capability comparison is provided in the Supplementary Material.

\subsection{Evidence boundaries in mouse-brain modelling}
\label{subsec:neurocomputational-boundaries}

Mouse-brain resources span incompatible resolutions. Allen tracer data and derived connectome models support mesoscale connectivity \citep{oh2014mesoscale,knox2019connectome}. The Virtual Mouse Brain supports connectome-based population dynamics, while \ac{bmtk} and Allen \ac{vone} models provide point-neuron and biophysical simulation \citep{melozzi2017virtualmouse,dai2020bmtk,billeh2020v1}. These platforms already solve network-construction and simulation problems. Reimplementing them would not address claim authorization.

Sensorium and Dynamic Sensorium evaluate neural-response prediction for images and videos, with later work extending stimulus coverage and population-response analysis \citep{willeke2022sensorium,turishcheva2023dynamic,turishcheva2024retrospective,wang2025foundation,liscai2025population}. Prediction is valuable but does not uniquely identify topology, biological direction, or cause. \ac{microns} combines electron-microscopy reconstruction and functional measurements in part of visual cortex \citep{bae2025microns,ding2025wiring}. It enables local observational structure--function analysis, not a measured whole-brain synaptic connectome.

Digital Twin Brain proposals and the current MouseDTB OpenReview submission combine inferred connectivity, neural dynamics, assimilation, and macroscopic validation \citep{xiong2023dtb,chen2026mousedtb}. They make it especially important to distinguish signal agreement, mechanistic identification, entity-specific calibration, causal intervention, anatomical coverage, independent validation, and operational compute. MouseClaimBench operates above these resources. Its novelty is the artifact-bound combination of domain claim knowledge, non-compensatory inference, proof traces, and evaluation at logical, synthetic, and real-case levels.
