\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

MouseClaimBench connects knowledge engineering, scientific claim verification, model assurance, and neurocomputational modelling. These areas operate on different objects. Knowledge engineering structures domain concepts and inference procedures. Claim verification links statements to documentary evidence. Assurance and model credibility determine whether evidence supports a declared use. Mouse-brain modelling constructs predictive, structural, and dynamical representations of a biological system. The proposed contribution is an executable connection between heterogeneous domain evidence and claim semantics.

\subsection{Knowledge engineering, rule systems, and provenance}
\label{subsec:knowledge-engineering}

Classical rule-based expert systems separate a knowledge base from an inference engine and make domain conclusions inspectable through the rules that fire \citep{buchanan1984rulebased}. Knowledge engineering later moved from direct transfer of expert statements towards explicit modelling of domain knowledge, problem-solving methods, and reusable assumptions \citep{studer1998knowledge}. This distinction is central to MouseClaimBench. Mouse-brain evidence requirements belong to a domain profile, while the conflict-resolution and explanation mechanisms belong to the reusable engine.

Formal ontologies specify concepts, relations, and constraints in a machine-processable form \citep{guarino1998formal}. Large semantic ontologies are not always necessary for bounded rule systems, but the separation between vocabulary and instances remains useful. The MouseClaimBench profile declares claim types, evidence-block types, evidence states, decision states, and rule relations. Each evaluated study supplies instances of evidence facts. The implementation exports this structure as a deterministic typed graph rather than relying on implicit control flow.

Provenance is equally important because a scientific conclusion is not auditable when its supporting observation has lost its origin. The W3C PROV family formalizes entities, activities, agents, and their derivations, and PROV-O provides an interoperable ontology for provenance descriptions \citep{lebo2013provo}. MouseClaimBench does not claim full PROV-O compliance. It applies the same design principle by binding each fact to a source path, predicate, rationale, named observations, artifact revision, and profile hash. This information is returned with the inference rather than stored only in a log.

Recent knowledge-based research combines ontologies, graphs, embeddings, and reasoning for scientific or biomedical tasks. ResearchLink represents scholarly entities and relations in a \ac{kg} and ranks plausible new research hypotheses \citep{borrego2025research}. Ontology-enriched causal graphs combine domain semantics and learned relations for interpretable biomedical inference \citep{pham2026causal}. These systems infer new links or hypotheses. MouseClaimBench instead performs claim authorization over observed evidence facts. Its rules are deliberately explicit because the negative conclusions and scope boundaries are part of the required output.

\subsection{Scientific claims, assurance, and reproducibility}
\label{subsec:claims-assurance}

Scientific claim verification commonly decomposes the task into document retrieval, rationale selection, and classification into support, contradiction, or insufficient information. SciFact established a benchmark of scientific claims linked to evidence-containing abstracts \citep{wadden2020scifact}. MultiVerS uses full-document context and weak supervision, while SciFact-Open exposes the additional difficulty of open-domain retrieval \citep{wadden2022multivers,wadden2022scifactopen}. Surveys identify domain transfer, incomplete knowledge, rationale quality, and evidence retrieval as continuing challenges \citep{vladika2023survey}.

Recent work broadens modality and grounding. SciTab evaluates compositional reasoning over scientific tables \citep{lu2023scitab}. SciVer and \ac{mever} combine text with figures or charts \citep{wang2025sciver,zhang2026mever}. SciTrue emphasizes source-level traceability for \ac{llm}-assisted verification, while PhyVer translates material claims into executable physical evidence \citep{tan2026scitrue,chen2026phyver}. MouseClaimBench differs in its decision object. It does not infer whether a passage textually supports a sentence. It tests whether a study contains the topology, directional, intervention, coverage, replication, and release evidence required by the claim type.

Selective prediction permits a model to abstain rather than force every input into one class \citep{geifman2019selectivenet}. A single reject state is insufficient for the present problem. Failed evidence, missing evidence, an untargeted protocol, and judgement outside automation require different explanations. The five-state rule base therefore preserves these conditions instead of mapping them to one confidence value. Language models may assist candidate discovery, but their uncertainty cannot create a missing intervention or external replication \citep{fadeeva2024factchecking}.

Model \ac{vv}, \ac{uq}, and assurance cases provide a complementary perspective. They connect evidence to a stated context of use and decompose high-level arguments into claims, evidence, assumptions, and defeaters \citep{oberkampf2010verification,sargent2013verification,riedmaier2021vvuq,asme2018vv40,batarseh2021assurance,mohamad2021assurance}. MouseClaimBench applies a narrower executable policy to scientific wording. Reproducibility standards, model cards, datasheets, and \ac{fair} guidance improve traceability and declared limitations \citep{wilkinson2016fair,mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets,pineau2021reproducibility,barker2022fair4rs}. They do not determine whether repeatable execution, within-resource reproduction, and external replication have all been achieved. These remain separate knowledge predicates.

\subsection{Evidence boundaries in mouse-brain modelling}
\label{subsec:neurocomputational-boundaries}

Mouse-brain resources span multiple resolutions. Allen tracer experiments and derived connectome models support mesoscale region and voxel connectivity \citep{oh2014mesoscale,knox2019connectome}. The Virtual Mouse Brain supports connectome-based population dynamics \citep{melozzi2017virtualmouse}. \ac{bmtk} and Allen \ac{vone} models support point-neuron and biophysically detailed simulation \citep{dai2020bmtk,billeh2020v1}. These platforms already address simulation and network construction. Reimplementing them would not solve the knowledge-representation problem considered here.

Sensorium and Dynamic Sensorium evaluate neural-response prediction for static images and videos \citep{willeke2022sensorium,turishcheva2023dynamic,turishcheva2024retrospective}. More recent models extend prediction across stimulus classes and examine population response geometry \citep{wang2025foundation,liscai2025population}. Predictive performance is scientifically useful, but it does not uniquely identify synaptic topology, biological direction, or causal mechanism. The knowledge profile consequently represents predictive and mechanistic claims through different evidence requirements.

\ac{microns} combines electron-microscopy reconstruction and functional measurements within part of mouse visual cortex \citep{bae2025microns,ding2025wiring}. It permits local structure--function analyses under spatial and network controls, but it does not provide a measured synaptic connectome for the complete mouse brain. Digital Twin Brain proposals and MouseDTB combine inferred connectivity, neural dynamics, data assimilation, and macroscopic validation \citep{xiong2023dtb,chen2026mousedtb}. These efforts reinforce the need to distinguish inferred connectivity, signal agreement, mechanistic identification, entity-specific validation, and causal intervention.

MouseClaimBench operates above these resources. It does not pool their biological fidelity into one score and does not replace their native analyses. It records the test performed, the evidence state produced, the knowledge rule fired, and the strongest bounded claim admitted by that rule. The novelty lies in the artifact-bound combination of domain claim knowledge, non-compensatory inference, proof traces, and validation at semantic, software, and real-case levels.
