\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

MouseClaimBench connects four research areas that normally evaluate different objects. Decision-support and design-science research evaluates artifacts that improve a decision process. Model credibility and assurance evaluate whether evidence supports a declared use or argument. Scientific claim verification evaluates relations between statements and documentary evidence. Neurocomputational modelling evaluates predictive, structural, and dynamical representations of a biological system. The proposed contribution lies in the executable connection among these objects, not in replacing the established methods within any one area.

\subsection{Decision support, model credibility, and assurance}
\label{subsec:credibility-assurance}

Design-science research requires an artifact, a relevant problem, a rigorous construction process, and an evaluation that demonstrates utility within a stated environment \citep{hevner2004design,peffers2007design}. Reviews of \ac{dss} research have repeatedly identified the need to connect technical artifacts to more general decision implications and to evaluate their use rather than report implementation alone \citep{arnott2005critical}. Earlier evidence-assembly systems also showed the value of representing support and uncertainty explicitly during decision making \citep{davis2003evidence}. This requirement is important for MouseClaimBench. Its executable gate can be validated technically, but its impact on authors and reviewers cannot be inferred without a human study.

Non-compensatory decision methods provide the formal intuition for the proposed policy. In compensatory aggregation, strong performance can offset a weak criterion. Outranking methods such as \ac{electre} permit veto conditions when some deficiencies should remain decisive \citep{figueira2013electre}. MouseClaimBench adopts the non-substitution principle, but it does not rank manuscripts or models. A veto applies only to the wording that requires the failed evidence block. A weaker claim may remain admissible.

\ac{vv} distinguishes correct implementation from adequacy for a context of use \citep{oberkampf2010verification,sargent2013verification}. \ac{vv} and \ac{uq} frameworks further relate credibility to assumptions, uncertainty, and available evidence \citep{riedmaier2021vvuq,asme2018vv40}. Assurance cases organize high-level statements through subclaims, argument patterns, evidence, and defeaters. Reviews show that decomposition is mature, while concrete evidence assignment, maintenance, and tooling remain uneven \citep{batarseh2021assurance,mohamad2021assurance}. Recent ethics-assurance patterns also emphasize traceability and challengeable evidence \citep{porter2024praise}. MouseClaimBench shares this structure but targets the admissible wording of empirical scientific results and executes predicates against repository artifacts.

Reproducibility is a necessary but separate concern. The \ac{fair} principles, \ac{fair} software guidance, model cards, datasheets, and machine-learning reproducibility programmes improve access to data, code, intended use, and limitations \citep{wilkinson2016fair,mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets,pineau2021reproducibility,barker2022fair4rs}. Biological modelling guidance extends these concerns towards understandable, reproducible, extensible, and reusable models \citep{patel2023fairbiors,sauro2026cure}. These practices strengthen an evidence package. They do not determine whether repeatability, within-resource confirmation, or independent replication has been achieved. MouseClaimBench represents these as separate blocks.

\subsection{Scientific claim verification and selective decisions}
\label{subsec:claim-verification}

Scientific claim verification commonly decomposes the task into document retrieval, rationale selection, and classification into support, contradiction, or insufficient information. SciFact established a benchmark of scientific claims connected to evidence-containing abstracts \citep{wadden2020scifact}. MultiVerS uses full-document context and weak supervision, while SciFact-Open exposes the additional difficulty of open-domain retrieval \citep{wadden2022multivers,wadden2022scifactopen}. Surveys identify domain transfer, incomplete knowledge, rationale quality, and evidence retrieval as continuing challenges \citep{vladika2023survey}.

Recent work broadens both modality and grounding. SciTab evaluates compositional reasoning over scientific tables \citep{lu2023scitab}. SciVer and \ac{mever} combine text with figures or charts \citep{wang2025sciver,zhang2026mever}. SciTrue focuses on source-level traceability for \ac{llm}-assisted scientific verification \citep{tan2026scitrue}. PhyVer is especially close in motivation because it translates material claims into executable physical evidence rather than relying only on textual entailment \citep{chen2026phyver}. MouseClaimBench differs in decision object. It does not predict whether a sentence is true from a passage. It evaluates whether a study contains the topology, directional, matched-control, intervention, coverage, replication, and release evidence required by the wording.

Selective prediction provides a second relevant idea. A model with a reject option can trade coverage for risk rather than force every input into a class \citep{geifman2019selectivenet}. Scientific claim governance also needs abstention, but a single reject state is insufficient. Missing evidence, failed evidence, untargeted evidence, and non-automatable judgement have different consequences. The v3 contract therefore produces five workflow dispositions. The legacy three-state perturbation analysis remains a numerical-stability diagnostic and is not presented as calibrated probability or as the workflow state model.

Language models may assist candidate discovery, decomposition, and attribution, but uncertainty and source quality remain decisive \citep{fadeeva2024factchecking,tan2026scitrue}. MouseClaimBench consequently treats deterministic rules or an optional \ac{llm} as candidate generators only. They cannot create evidence, alter a contract, or authorize a statement. This separation makes the final decision deterministic for a fixed artifact package.

\subsection{Evidence boundaries in mouse-brain modelling}
\label{subsec:neurocomputational-boundaries}

Mouse-brain resources span multiple resolutions. Allen tracer experiments and derived connectome models support mesoscale region and voxel connectivity \citep{oh2014mesoscale,knox2019connectome}. The Virtual Mouse Brain supports connectome-based population dynamics \citep{melozzi2017virtualmouse}. \ac{bmtk} and Allen \ac{vone} models support point-neuron and biophysically detailed simulation \citep{dai2020bmtk,billeh2020v1}. These platforms already address simulation and network construction. Reimplementing them would not solve the interpretation problem considered here.

Sensorium and Dynamic Sensorium evaluate neural-response prediction for static images and videos \citep{willeke2022sensorium,turishcheva2023dynamic,turishcheva2024retrospective}. More recent models extend prediction across stimulus classes and investigate population response geometry \citep{wang2025foundation,liscai2025population}. Predictive performance is scientifically useful, but it does not uniquely identify synaptic topology, biological direction, or causal mechanism. This distinction motivates the predictive and mechanistic blocks in the contract.

\ac{microns} combines electron-microscopy reconstruction and functional measurements within part of mouse visual cortex \citep{bae2025microns,ding2025wiring}. It permits local structure--function analyses under spatial and network controls. It does not provide a measured synaptic connectome for the complete mouse brain. At a broader scale, Digital Twin Brain proposals and MouseDTB combine inferred connectivity, neural dynamics, data assimilation, and macroscopic validation \citep{xiong2023dtb,chen2026mousedtb}. MouseDTB demonstrates a substantial whole-brain single-neuron-resolution modelling effort. It also reinforces the need to distinguish inferred connectivity, signal agreement, mechanistic identification, entity-specific validation, and causal intervention.

MouseClaimBench operates above these resources. It does not compare their biological fidelity on one common scale. It records which domain-specific test was performed and which statement that test can support. Table~\ref{tab:related-work-comparison} summarizes the distinction. The novelty is therefore narrower than a general scientific verifier and broader than a mouse-specific metric. It is an executable \ac{dss} policy that connects heterogeneous evidence, bounded wording, and reproducible release decisions.
