Scientific Artificial Intelligence systems increasingly support predictive models, digital representations, and mechanistic interpretations in data-intensive domains. However, standard evaluation pipelines usually report predictive scores, reproducibility checks, or benchmark rankings without helping authors and reviewers decide whether the scientific claims attached to those results are actually supported. This paper presents ClaimBench, an executable decision-support framework for scientific AI claim governance. ClaimBench represents claims as explicit contracts and evaluates them through non-compensatory gates that separate prediction, evidence retrieval, topology specificity, causal direction, uncertainty, manuscript wording, cost, and release reproducibility. The framework includes deterministic adversarial tests, external controls based on SciFact and the Tuebingen cause-effect benchmark, component ablations, a reviewer-oriented threat model, and a non-authoritative LLM-ready layer for candidate claim extraction. The LLM layer does not authorize claims and does not call an external API in the reproducible configuration. It only proposes candidate statements that must be validated by executable artifacts. Across the current release, the reproduction package passes 15 of 15 stages, the unified report passes 10 of 10 criteria, and the release check reports no missing, dirty, or failing artifacts. The results support ClaimBench as a decision-support layer for auditing scientific AI claims before submission or review. They do not support claims of state-of-the-art prediction, causal discovery performance, or complete biological digital twins.
