\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The evaluation supports MouseClaimBench as a bounded \ac{kbs} for computational mouse-brain claims. Its contribution is not a new simulator, predictive model, fact checker, or causal-discovery algorithm. It is an explicit representation of the knowledge required by a claim and a deterministic connection between that knowledge and source evidence. This distinction matters because prediction, computational reproducibility, topology specificity, direction, structure--function association, intervention, and biological scope answer different scientific questions.

\subsection{Knowledge-system interpretation and lessons learned}
\label{subsec:kbs-lessons}

The first lesson concerns separation of domain knowledge from case facts. The profile declares that a mechanistic claim requires prediction, internal reproduction, topology specificity, and direction. Allen supplies case facts for those requirements. Its internal-reproduction fact passes, while topology and direction fail. The engine does not need Allen-specific control flow to reach the blocked conclusion. This separation makes the domain assumptions visible and allows the same inference procedure to evaluate heterogeneous cases.

The second lesson is that non-compensation must be an explicit rule property. The prediction shortcut obtains a low \ac{fnr} because it promotes one successful block to several stronger interpretations. It also creates 785 false authorizations under the oracle. Equation~\ref{eq:five-state-gate} prevents this substitution. The result is not universal rejection. Ten real-case claims remain supported, including prediction, internal reproduction, topology specificity, and local structure--function association.

The third lesson is that an explanation has two distinct validity requirements. Computational fidelity asks whether the trace accurately represents the rule execution. MouseClaimBench demonstrates this through 40 exact decision matches, 40 complete traces, source facts, witness blocks, and profile hashes. Scientific adequacy asks whether the domain requirement and source predicate are appropriate. The conformance audit cannot answer that second question because it uses the declared policy as its reference. Reporting both levels prevents explainable execution from being misrepresented as biological validation.

The fourth lesson is that negative conclusions are knowledge outputs rather than system failures. Allen supplies a negative mechanistic case. Tuebingen blocks causal-direction performance. The \ac{microns} case supports a local observational association while preserving the absence of intervention and external replication. A global score would tend to hide either the valid positive evidence or the decisive limitation. The five-state inference preserves both.

The fifth lesson concerns the meaning of generality. The software can load a different validated profile, and the graph schema does not contain hard-coded Allen, Sensorium, or \ac{microns} nodes. This is architectural extensibility. It is not cross-domain empirical generality. Another domain would require its own claim vocabulary, evidence predicates, priorities, and validation cases. Keeping the present paper mouse-specific avoids a shallow demonstration whose only purpose is to claim breadth.

The sixth lesson concerns optional language models. An \ac{llm} can propose candidate statements or map paraphrases to known claim types. It cannot create a fact, alter the profile hash, or satisfy an absent intervention. This is a useful design boundary because extraction models can change without changing authorization semantics. The final inference remains reproducible for the same knowledge profile and evidence package.

Table~\ref{tab:decision-actions} summarizes the knowledge implication of each conclusion. A supported state means that the declared requirements pass. It does not certify truth beyond the profile and source predicates. Blocked, uncertain, out-of-scope, and review states retain distinct reasons that can be inspected and contested.

\subsection{Relationship to existing knowledge systems and applicability}
\label{subsec:comparative-discussion}

MouseClaimBench follows the classical separation of knowledge base and inference engine, but its target differs from diagnosis or configuration systems. The inferred object is the admissible scope of a scientific statement. Its facts are generated by heterogeneous computational protocols rather than entered as homogeneous symbolic observations. Provenance is therefore part of the inference result rather than optional metadata.

The method also differs from scientific knowledge graphs used for discovery. ResearchLink predicts plausible missing scholarly relations from graph, textual, and bibliometric features \citep{borrego2025research}. MouseClaimBench does not predict new relations. Its graph records why a declared claim is supported or blocked. This narrower reasoning task permits explicit rules and deterministic proof traces, while discovery systems require learned ranking and broader candidate coverage.

Scientific claim verifiers estimate whether documents or evidence spans support a textual proposition \citep{wadden2020scifact,wadden2022multivers,wang2025sciver,tan2026scitrue}. MouseClaimBench begins after an evidence artifact and domain predicate have been identified. The SciFact control confirms that retrieval and support classification are already different operations. The knowledge profile adds another boundary by asking whether textual or numerical support is the right evidence type for the claim being made.

Assurance cases and model-credibility frameworks remain broader in organizational scope \citep{oberkampf2010verification,batarseh2021assurance,mohamad2021assurance}. They can include context, rebuttals, hazards, stakeholder responsibilities, and certification arguments. MouseClaimBench operationalizes a smaller claim-to-evidence layer with executable predicates. It should be viewed as a component that can feed a larger assurance process rather than a replacement for one.

Within computational neuroscience, the immediate use is comparison of claim scope across resources that cannot be normalized honestly. Allen, Sensorium, and \ac{microns} each provide strong evidence for different questions. The knowledge system preserves those differences and records why a resource that supports one claim does not support another. This is particularly relevant when terms such as mechanism, causality, and digital twin are used across incompatible spatial scales and validation protocols.

\subsection{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{subsec:limitations}

Construct validity depends on the mouse-brain claim vocabulary and source predicates. The profile is explicit and versioned, but it is still a methodological proposal created within this project. Other researchers may dispute whether one evidence block is necessary, whether another is missing, or whether a threshold is appropriate. The current results show the consequences of the declared policy. They do not establish a unique ontology of scientific evidence.

The oracle benchmark reduces the circularity of the legacy suite because its graph and intervention labels are fixed independently of the contract thresholds. Its five regimes remain low dimensional. Their nonlinearities, noise levels, cohort sizes, and controls cover only a subset of identification problems. The case-level sign test supports a policy difference under this generator. It does not prove optimality under arbitrary data-generating processes.

The real cases are an evidence audit rather than a representative sample of mouse-brain studies. They were selected because they provide complementary predictive, structural, directional, and reproducibility states. Exact conformance to the frozen matrix demonstrates migration fidelity, not external biological validity. A stronger test would freeze a profile version before applying it to studies and resources selected outside the present development process.

The \ac{microns} endpoint comes from one cortical volume. Non-overlapping windows provide internal reproduction, not replication across animals or laboratories. The unit-cluster weighted bootstrap addresses reuse of neurons within the directed-pair frame, but it is not a complete inferential solution for network-dependent data. Comparison with dyadic or graph-dependent variance estimators remains necessary. The endpoint was fixed after discovery, so two hold-outs reduce but do not remove selection risk.

The external controls do not establish task competitiveness. SciFact uses transparent local baselines, and the Tuebingen proxies perform below established causal methods. Their purpose is to test whether retrieval or association can satisfy stronger evidence requirements. Future work can integrate modern verifiers and causal methods as evidence producers, but stronger upstream models do not remove the need for claim-specific knowledge rules.

The proof trace is computationally faithful but has not been evaluated for human comprehensibility. This paper does not claim reduced review time, improved agreement, better revisions, or improved human decisions. Such outcomes are outside the stated KBS evaluation. The system also does not determine novelty, importance, ethics, or publication acceptance.

Finally, the exported graph is a typed operational graph rather than a full OWL ontology or PROV-O serialization. Its current purpose is deterministic inspection of claims, requirements, rules, states, and sources. Standards-based semantic interchange, rule-evolution analysis, contradiction handling across profiles, and profile alignment remain future engineering work. These extensions should preserve the current profile hash and inference trace so that added interoperability does not weaken reproducibility.
