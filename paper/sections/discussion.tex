\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The evaluation supports MouseClaimBench as a bounded decision-support method. Its contribution is not a new neuroscience simulator, fact-checking model, or causal-discovery algorithm. It is an executable separation between evidence that is available and wording that is admissible. That separation matters because prediction, reproducible computation, topology specificity, direction, structure--function association, intervention, and biological scope answer different questions. The oracle benchmark shows that a predictive shortcut can achieve a very low \ac{fnr} while authorizing many unsupported interpretations. The four mouse cases then show that the stricter policy does not simply reject everything. It retains prediction for Sensorium, internal reproduction for Allen and Sensorium, topology specificity for Static Sensorium, and a local observational association for \ac{microns}.

\subsection{Decision-support interpretation and lessons learned}
\label{subsec:decision-support-and-lessons}

The system supports an editorial decision about one candidate statement. The available actions are to retain bounded wording, weaken it, mark it as uncertain, exclude it from the evaluated scope, or request external judgement. MouseClaimBench does not recommend accepting or rejecting a paper. It exposes the observations, rules, sources, and failed blocks that determine the disposition. The first lesson is that computational reproducibility is necessary but not mechanistic evidence. Allen is the clearest example. Its target is stable across mice and split halves, yet the declared topology controls fail and the directional diagnostics carry no signal. A single quality score would either hide this failure or discard the valid reproducibility result. The contract retains both conclusions. The same reasoning applies to \ac{microns}. Reproducing an observational association in non-overlapping windows of one volume strengthens internal stability, but it does not create an intervention or an independent biological replication.

The second lesson is that non-compensation has practical value only when the evidence requirements are explicit and contestable. Equation~\ref{eq:five-state-gate} prevents a successful block from compensating for a failed mandatory block. It cannot establish that the block itself is scientifically appropriate. Domain experts must justify the endpoint, control, threshold, and scope. This division of labour is important for a \ac{dss}. The software makes a declared policy consistent and auditable, while scientific authority remains with qualified researchers and reviewers.

The third lesson is that error control must be paired with coverage. The oracle benchmark reduces \ac{fpr} from $0.271$ to $0.019$, but its \ac{fnr} is higher than the shortcut value. The calibrated SciFact baseline shows the same trade-off more strongly. Its low false-authorization rate accompanies a \ac{fnr} of $0.718$. A claim-governance system should not optimize conservativeness alone. It should report both rates, expose abstention, and state the decision cost that motivates a threshold. This is why the manuscript uses standard \ac{fpr} and \ac{fnr} terminology instead of presenting renamed ratios as novel metrics.

The fourth lesson is that negative and inconclusive results are useful decision artifacts. The Allen negative case, the weak Tuebingen direction result, and the globally bounded \ac{microns} interpretation all prevent stronger claims without invalidating the underlying datasets. This behaviour differs from a leaderboard, where every component is expected to raise one aggregate score. In MouseClaimBench, a blocked disposition can be the correct and informative output.

The fifth lesson concerns \acp{llm}. A language model may improve candidate coverage, cluster similar statements, or suggest a weaker formulation. It cannot supply an absent intervention, independent replication, or whole-brain validation. The implementation therefore keeps claim discovery outside authorization. This boundary also improves reproducibility because the evidence policy remains stable when the extraction model or prompt changes.

\subsection{Relationship to existing systems and practical applicability}
\label{subsec:comparative-discussion}

MouseClaimBench occupies a narrower position than scientific fact checking. SciFact, MultiVerS, SciVer, SciTrue, and related systems estimate whether a document or evidence span supports a textual proposition \citep{wadden2020scifact,wadden2022multivers,wang2025sciver,tan2026scitrue}. MouseClaimBench begins after potentially relevant evidence has been located. It asks whether that evidence type authorizes a specific scientific interpretation. The SciFact experiment confirms that these are separate decisions. Retrieval recall@5 can approach $0.90$ while local support rules still produce substantial false authorization or false rejection.

The method is also narrower than a formal assurance case. Assurance arguments connect claims, evidence, context, and rebuttals in safety-critical systems \citep{batarseh2021assurance,mohamad2021assurance}. MouseClaimBench operationalizes a small part of that logic for manuscript wording. Its blocks are executable and artifact-bound, but the framework does not provide a complete safety argument, organizational governance process, or certification decision. Conversely, model reporting and reproducibility standards improve transparency without deciding whether one successful metric licenses a causal or mechanistic interpretation \citep{pineau2021reproducibility,barker2022fair4rs}.

For decision-support research, the relevant design claim is therefore modest. MouseClaimBench transforms heterogeneous validation outputs into a traceable five-state disposition while preserving veto conditions and human authority. It can be used during author preparation, internal review, and response to reviewers. Its artifact binding is particularly useful when numerical results remain fixed but wording expands during revision. A conventional unit test will not identify that interpretive drift. A contract can record the permitted and blocked formulations beside the result that determines them.

The current domain profile is specific to computational mouse-brain modelling. It distinguishes predictive visual-response evidence from mesoscopic topology, directed identification, local structure--function association, intervention, and whole-brain scope. The policy engine can be reused elsewhere, but the contracts cannot be transferred unchanged. Clinical, fairness, safety, and deployment claims require their own expert-defined endpoints, controls, and decision costs. Generality lies in the representation and audit mechanism, not in a universal list of scientific thresholds.

\subsection{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{subsec:limitations}

Internal validity is limited by the generated benchmark. Its reference labels come from declared structural equations and intervention availability rather than from the contract thresholds, which removes the circularity of the legacy suite. However, the five regimes remain low dimensional. Their nonlinearities, noise levels, cohort size, and available controls cover only a small subset of plausible identification problems. The case-level sign test supports a difference between two policies under this generator. It does not prove that the contract is optimal, universally calibrated, or superior to every selective-decision method.

Construct validity depends on the claim taxonomy and source-specific predicates. The five evidence states distinguish failure, absence, non-applicability, and external review, but expert readers may dispute whether a particular endpoint belongs in a block or whether its threshold is appropriate. That disagreement is not hidden by a learned score. It must be resolved through protocol revision, versioning, and independent adjudication. The legacy v2 suite cannot answer this question because its labels instantiate the same semantics as the tested implementation.

The real mouse cases have deliberately different scopes. Allen and Sensorium contain multiple mice, while the \ac{microns} endpoint is drawn from one cortical volume and uses non-overlapping analytical windows rather than independent animals or laboratories. The unit-cluster weighted bootstrap addresses reuse of neurons within the directed-pair frame. It is an analysis of stability, not a full inferential solution for dependent network data. Comparison with dyadic or graph-dependent variance estimators remains necessary. The fixed \ac{microns} endpoint was selected after discovery, so the two hold-outs reduce but do not eliminate selection risk.

The external controls do not establish task competitiveness. The SciFact models are transparent local baselines and the Tuebingen proxies perform below established causal methods. Their purpose is to exhibit failure modes under real data. A stronger evaluation should compare the decision policy with modern claim verification, selective prediction, causal discovery, and uncertainty baselines under a shared claim-level protocol. It should also include distribution shift and studies designed outside the project team.

The most important missing evidence concerns people. The study package specifies 50 items and a 24-participant pilot, but no ethics approval, recruitment, adjudicated corpus, or outcome data currently exists. Consequently, the paper cannot claim reduced review time, improved agreement, better revisions, or better calibrated expert decisions. Those outcomes determine whether the method is useful as a human-facing \ac{dss}. The present evidence supports technical readiness for that study, not its result.

Finally, the framework does not determine novelty, importance, ethics, or publication acceptance. Candidate discovery can miss paraphrases and any \ac{llm}-assisted extractor can vary across models and prompts. Artifact integrity can show which code produced a result, but it cannot repair biased data or an invalid experiment. MouseClaimBench should therefore complement expert review. Treating its output as an automated scientific verdict would violate the authority boundary on which the method is based.
