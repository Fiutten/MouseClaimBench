\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support MouseClaimBench as a bounded knowledge-based authorization layer. They do not support a claim that the system determines scientific truth. Its principal function is to prevent a valid result in one evidential dimension from being promoted into a stronger conclusion that requires different evidence. This distinction is operational rather than rhetorical because authorization is computed from named requirements, package integrity, and complete deficit traces.

\subsection{Scientific interpretation and methodological novelty}
\label{subsec:interpretation-novelty}

The standards experiment provides an important negative baseline. A third-party \ac{shacl} engine reproduces every structural decision and deficit. MouseClaimBench therefore cannot claim novelty for checking required graph fields, representing provenance, or exporting JSON-LD. These capabilities are established by \ac{shacl}, \ac{prov}, and the broader semantic-web ecosystem. Similarly, assurance cases already represent claims and evidence, while scientific verification already retrieves and classifies documentary support.

The defensible contribution is the authorization-contract architecture that remains after established components are removed from the novelty claim. It jointly binds domain evidence semantics, structural admissibility, provenance integrity, and complete non-compensatory deficits. The profile distinguishes prediction, reproduction, topology, direction, intervention, local structure--function association, and digital-twin scope. The admissibility rule rejects incomplete passing facts, while the integrity layer evaluates relations among artifacts, cohorts, attestations, and the profile itself. Python and \ac{shacl} agree on the complete structural benchmark, and \ac{asp} agrees with Python on its deterministic subset. The knowledge ledger makes every policy relation and predicate boundary inspectable. Structural sensitivity quantifies dependence on that policy, while counterfactual tests establish that the generated explanations are faithful to it. Controlled attacks and trust-boundary controls distinguish detectable inconsistency from unauthenticated but internally coherent metadata. Finally, frozen external protocols demonstrate that the system can retain a negative availability result and authorize a separate positive result without modifying the contract.

The sensitivity result is asymmetric in a useful but limited way. Fifty-nine of the 60 relation removals do not change an observed authorization because their associated cases contain additional deficits. This does not make those requirements redundant in the profile. It only means that they are not singly decisive in the fixed cases. The remaining removal identifies data quality as the sole barrier in Dynamic Sensorium. Conversely, adding unprovided requirements contracts the four positive bounded cases. The analysis therefore reveals where decisions depend on current policy scope, but it supplies no empirical criterion for choosing a more permissive or more restrictive profile.

The explanation study supports a narrower statement than human usefulness. Full repair is sufficient, every repaired deficit is individually necessary, and the witnesses remain complete. A single-reason trace would discard 41,641 simultaneous deficits in the generated packages. These are formal information and counterfactual properties. Whether the additional information improves reviewer accuracy, time, confidence, or agreement remains unknown.

The attack benchmark is stronger than a demonstration with a few hand-selected failures because it now covers every composition of the eight declared invariants for every claim. It is still narrower than a general security evaluation. The zero false authorizations across 2,550 attacked packages and exact combined traces are conditional on those invariants. More importantly, all 20 coherent-forgery controls escape. An attacker who changes content and its asserted hash together or supplies false but internally consistent independence metadata leaves no contradiction for the gate to observe. The result therefore documents both controlled robustness and a concrete failure boundary. Source trust, digital signatures, archive compromise, and unmodelled dependence relations remain outside the current gate.

The prospective applications strengthen temporal validity but should not be confused with independent discovery. The DANDI:000039 contrast response is biologically expected and the Ridge model is intentionally simple. Its role is to test whether a precommitted data selection, endpoint, comparator, uncertainty calculation, and authorization rule can survive contact with an unseen asset catalog. DANDI:001176 reveals the complementary failure mode. Metadata listed 29 subjects, but only 5 supplied the simultaneous schema required by the frozen endpoint. Lowering the minimum after this observation would produce a more convenient experiment and weaker evidence.

The \ac{microns} result is also deliberately narrow. Directed dyadic covariance increases the discovery standard error from the naive HC1 value by a factor of 1.785, which confirms that pair-level independence would be anti-conservative. Node-label permutations retain the positive association in all three windows. This supports the declared local observational endpoint. It does not establish a synaptic mechanism, external replication, or a complete visual-cortex model.

\paragraph{Lessons learned.}\label{par:lessons-learned}
Four practical lessons follow. Structural conformance should be delegated to a standard engine whenever possible. Provenance must include dependence and independence claims, not only file locations. Negative outcomes should remain first-class release artifacts because they expose unavailable evidence and prevent silent protocol repair. Finally, a complete deficit set retains substantially more policy-relevant information than one decisive label when multiple requirements fail simultaneously. Its practical value to human reviewers has not been measured.

\subsection{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{subsec:limitations}

Construct validity remains the principal limitation. Profile v2 is author-defined and literature-grounded. Its 10 claims, 22 predicate contracts, 60 direct relations, claim-specific rationales, exceptions, and sources are now fully inspectable. Structural sensitivity also makes decision dependence visible. However, no independent expert panel or community process has established that the claims, predicates, or relations are complete. The frozen v2 external-review protocol contains 92 items but has zero completed independent raters. Soundness, completeness, counterfactual fidelity, and sensitivity are relative to this profile. They cannot convert an author-defined policy into a consensus scientific taxonomy.

Internal validity is stronger for software behaviour than for scientific content. Expected outcomes in the mutation, counterfactual, and declared-attack benchmarks follow directly from the authorization semantics. Multiple implementations reduce the risk of a coding error, but Python, \ac{asp}, and \ac{shacl} still share the same profile specification. The 221 structural variants expose local decision sensitivity and do not span the space of scientifically plausible profiles. The attack generator is deterministic and non-adaptive, and the coherent-forgery controls confirm that self-consistent false metadata remains unobservable. Property checks cover sampled packages rather than every possible future profile and package graph.

The real applications are heterogeneous and should not be pooled as independent replications. Allen, Sensorium, IBL, \ac{microns}, and DANDI answer different questions. The first five-package mapping reuses artifacts from the preceding MouseBrainBench study and provides retrospective contract application rather than new data validation. The \ac{microns} windows occupy one cortical volume. The two DANDI studies use different measurements and cannot replicate one another. DANDI:000039 validates a bounded predictive contract with a simple comparator, not model superiority. DANDI:001176 is an availability failure rather than an estimate of predictive performance.

Statistical uncertainty is handled at the declared independent unit where possible. The DANDI bootstrap resamples mice, and \ac{microns} uses directed dyadic covariance plus node-label permutation. These methods still depend on their exchangeability and model assumptions. The \ac{microns} hold-outs were fixed after discovery rather than externally preregistered, and all three use the same resource. The DANDI protocols were frozen in Git before numerical asset access but were not registered in a third-party registry.

External validity is not established outside computational mouse-brain evidence. The architecture can load another profile, but architectural extensibility is not empirical generality. Human comprehensibility, decision quality, review time, and inter-rater agreement were not evaluated. Runtime was measured on one Apple arm64 computer and excludes archive download time and external \ac{shacl} validation. These restrictions are encoded in the release gate and should remain visible in any subsequent use.
