\section{Integrity-Aware Knowledge-Based Method}
\label{sec:method}

MouseClaimBench receives a versioned authorization profile, a set of evidence blocks, and a manifest of the artifacts from which those blocks were produced. It returns a bounded decision, the complete set of unmet requirements, and a provenance-linked explanation. Fig.~\ref{fig:standards-workflow} shows the complete path. The three validation layers are intentionally separate because a well-formed graph can contain incoherent provenance, and an internally coherent package can still lack the scientific evidence required by a claim.

\subsection{Authorization profile and evidence semantics}
\label{subsec:authorization-semantics}

The knowledge profile is represented as
\begin{equation}
\Pi=\langle C,E,Q,O,V,H\rangle,
\label{eq:profile-v2}
\end{equation}
where $C$ is the claim vocabulary, $E$ is the evidence-block vocabulary, $Q(c)\subseteq E$ contains the mandatory blocks for claim $c$, $O(e)$ contains the observations required when block $e$ declares a pass, $V$ is the profile version, and $H$ is its SHA-256 content identity. The evaluated profile contains 10 claim types, 22 evidence-block types, and 60 claim-to-evidence requirements. Table~\ref{tab:profile-v2-claims} enumerates the claim types, while Fig.~\ref{fig:claim-evidence-matrix-v2} shows every required relation. The strict digital-twin claim directly requires 21 blocks. It does not inherit authorization from the other nine claims and is not a synonym for predictive performance.

The profile content follows a recorded acquisition procedure rather than being inferred from the conformance benchmark. The procedure first defines bounded claim wording and prohibited promotions. It then identifies distinct evidence functions from methodological and mouse-brain sources, records scope and exceptions, resolves the internal adversarial audit, and verifies exact executable and bibliographic coverage. The machine-readable basis contains one stable record and one claim-specific necessity rationale for each of the 60 direct relations. It also retains the status \emph{source-grounded author requirement} and \emph{not independently validated} for every relation. The source identifiers justify the methodological function of an evidence block. They are not represented as having prescribed the complete profile.

The separate external-review protocol adapts established content-validity procedures \citep{lynn1986content,terwee2018cosmin}. It requires at least seven independent experts, at least five years of relevant experience, representation of three expertise strata, complete 1--4 ratings, item- and scale-level content-validity indices, binary Fleiss agreement, and resolution of every critical veto. The acceptance thresholds were frozen before recruitment. No eligible rater has completed the protocol, so none of these planned statistics enters the present evidence.

Predicate ownership is recorded separately from claim authorization. Each of the 22 predicate contracts states whether an upstream source adapter, a release adapter, or the authorization adapter evaluates the scientific or operational rule. The contracts expose 124 required observation slots. Source adapters calculate endpoint-specific statuses and preserve the rule, rationale, observations, and artifact identity. The core authorization engine consumes those attestations, checks their schema and provenance admissibility, and applies $Q(c)$. It does not impose one universal numerical threshold across prediction, topology, intervention, anatomical coverage, and reproducibility.

An evidence block is
\begin{equation}
b_e=\langle e,a,x,s,\phi,r\rangle,
\label{eq:evidence-block-v2}
\end{equation}
where $a\in\{P,F,U,N,J\}$ denotes passed, failed, unknown, not targeted, or requires review, $x$ preserves the original-scale observations, $s$ identifies the source artifact, $\phi$ is the predicate executed by the declared source adapter, and $r$ is its rationale. The authorization engine validates the attested predicate record rather than recomputing every source analysis. It does not normalize correlation, matched-pair differences, software revisions, interventions, and anatomical coverage into a shared numerical scale. Let
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{present}(z)=
\begin{cases}
1, & z\text{ exists and is non-empty},\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:field-presence}
\end{equation}

A declared pass is admissible only when the block also contains its mandatory observation fields:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{adm}_{\Pi}(b_e)=
\mathrm{1}[a=P]\operatorname{present}(s)\operatorname{present}(\phi)
\operatorname{present}(r)\prod_{o\in O(e)}\operatorname{present}(x_o).
\label{eq:admissibility}
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathrm{1}[\cdot]$ is the indicator function. Consequently, failed, unknown, not targeted, and requires-review states are all non-admissible. A declared pass is also non-admissible when its source, predicate, rationale, or a required observation is absent. This rule prevents an evidence source from asserting \emph{passed} while omitting the target, split, comparator, inferential unit, uncertainty method, or other observations required by the profile. The complete scientific deficit set is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\Pi}(c,B)=\{e\in Q(c):\operatorname{adm}_{\Pi}(b_e)=0\},
\label{eq:scientific-deficits}
\end{equation}
where an absent block is materialized as unknown. Core authorization is non-compensatory:
\begin{equation}
A_{\Pi}(c,B)=\mathrm{1}[c\in C]\mathrm{1}[\Delta_{\Pi}(c,B)=\varnothing].
\label{eq:core-authorization}
\end{equation}
The engine reports every member of $\Delta_{\Pi}$ with declared and effective states, source, predicate, rationale, observations, and missing fields. It does not select one prioritized explanation or calculate a paper-level score. Category-complete reporting means that every violated contract dimension is retained without priority masking and has an actionable witness. It does not require exhaustive enumeration of every redundant witness or every distinct cycle within one violation category. Let $T_R(B)$ replace exactly the blocks in a reported repair set $R$ with admissible passing attestations. A category-complete deficit explanation is counterfactually sufficient and individually necessary when
\begin{equation}
A_{\Pi}(c,T_{\Delta_{\Pi}(c,B)}(B))=1
\quad\land\quad
\forall e\in\Delta_{\Pi}(c,B),\quad
A_{\Pi}(c,T_{\Delta_{\Pi}(c,B)\setminus\{e\}}(B))=0.
\label{eq:counterfactual-explanation}
\end{equation}
This property establishes fidelity to the non-compensatory profile. It does not establish that a human reviewer finds the explanation useful.

\subsection{Standards representation and package integrity}
\label{subsec:standards-integrity}

The profile and evidence package are exported as \ac{rdf} using \ac{prov}. Profiles, claims, evidence blocks, artifacts, activities, and decisions receive stable identifiers. Artifact derivation uses PROV relations, while profile-specific predicates represent mandatory evidence and authorization status. The same graph can be serialized as Turtle or JSON-LD. This layer supports exchange and inspection. It does not replace the executable profile.

For each profile, MouseClaimBench generates \ac{shacl} shapes that require the package and profile identity, all mandatory block records, a status from the allowed vocabulary, source, rule, rationale, and the observations required when a block declares a pass. A third-party \texttt{pySHACL} processor validates the resulting graph and returns structural deficits. The shapes do not require \texttt{passed}. A complete block with \texttt{failed} status is therefore structurally conformant but scientifically non-admissible. This distinction permits $S_{\Pi}=1$ and $A_{\Pi}=0$ for the same package. \ac{shacl} provides an external structural baseline. It does not authorize a scientific claim or make schema validation a new contribution. In this release, the structural contract applies to graphs emitted by the MouseClaimBench serializer from typed evidence-package objects. It is not presented as a complete validator for arbitrary hostile third-party \ac{rdf} graphs, where duplicated or contradictory singleton values would require additional uniqueness constraints.

The package-integrity layer evaluates relations that cannot be reduced to the presence of individual fields. Let $M$ contain the profile identity, content-addressed artifact records, derivation edges, block-to-artifact lineage, evidence attestations, and declared independence relations. Its integrity deficit set $I_{\Pi}(M,B)$ covers the 13 conditions in Table~\ref{tab:integrity-threat-model}. Artifact identifiers and block-lineage declarations must be unique. All artifact references from derivations, block lineage, attestations, independence pairs, and disjoint-cohort pairs must resolve. Block lineage and attestations must reference supplied evidence blocks. Every supplied block requires lineage and at least one attestation, while each direct attestation must agree with the represented block status. Independence and cohort-disjointness relations must be irreflexive. The taxonomy also protects contract identity, artifact content, derivation order, and declared independence. These assets follow established concerns in secure provenance, assurance cases, and the avoidance of pseudoreplication \citep{hasan2009provenance,mohamad2021assurance,lazic2010pseudoreplication}.

The cycle check uses an iterative depth-first traversal, which avoids recursion limits for large packages. Independence is evaluated from content identity and from the composite identity $(\texttt{study\_id},\texttt{data\_generation\_id})$ when both fields are non-empty. The implementation does not assume that \texttt{data\_generation\_id} is globally unique across studies. These checks establish internal package consistency under the declared metadata. They do not prove that a source is trustworthy or that two laboratories are scientifically independent. Profile identity is intentionally checked at both the structural graph boundary and the manifest-integrity boundary. The structural check prevents a graph from masquerading as an instance of a different executable profile, while the integrity check preserves the same invariant in the package manifest. This defense-in-depth overlap is reported explicitly rather than treated as evidence that the two gates are disjoint.

Structural conformance remains an independently executed gate. The graph is deterministically derived from the same claim, evidence blocks, and manifest evaluated by the other gates:
\begin{equation}
G_{\Pi}(c,B,M)=\operatorname{RDF}_{\Pi}(c,B,M).
\label{eq:package-rdf}
\end{equation}
Let $S_{\Pi}(G)=1$ exactly when external \ac{shacl} validation reports no structural violation against the shapes generated from $\Pi$. The field checks in $S_{\Pi}$ intentionally overlap with defensive admissibility checks in the Python core, but $S_{\Pi}$ is not absorbed into $A_{\Pi}$. Final authorization requires structural conformance, a complete scientific contract, and an integrity-clean package:
\begin{equation}
A^{*}_{\Pi}(c,B,M)=S_{\Pi}(G_{\Pi}(c,B,M))A_{\Pi}(c,B)
\mathrm{1}[I_{\Pi}(M,B)=\varnothing].
\label{eq:integrity-authorization}
\end{equation}
No control can compensate for another. A dedicated final authorization object executes this equation and preserves separate structural, domain, and integrity traces. Every reported deficit retains its corresponding witness.

\subsection{Independent execution, domain-gate property checks, and release control}
\label{subsec:execution-release}

The authorization semantics are implemented through three execution paths with deliberately different scopes. Source-specific Python adapters execute the scientific predicates and construct attested evidence blocks. The primary Python engine evaluates field presence, observation admissibility, domain requirements, and detailed scientific explanations. The external \ac{shacl} path evaluates only the structural contract encoded in the \ac{rdf} graph. A separately implemented \ac{asp} program receives declared statuses, metadata-presence facts, and observation-presence facts. It derives effective states, authorization, and deficits within Clingo for every generated domain-contract case. Relational package integrity is evaluated by its own Python component before the three gate results are composed. The paths share the profile specification but not the same inference code. Agreement between Python and \ac{asp}, and conformance of \ac{shacl} to structural expectations, provide implementation evidence only within their declared scopes. They do not validate the shared scientific policy.

The domain-gate property contract contains six profile-relative properties of $A_{\Pi}$. Soundness requires every domain-authorized claim to have all required admissible blocks. Completeness requires every package with all required admissible blocks to be domain-authorized. Deficit identity requires the reported set to equal the set defined in Eq.~\ref{eq:scientific-deficits}. Monotonic degradation requires replacing a passed block with a failed block to preserve or enlarge the deficit set and never create domain authorization. Domain decisions must also be invariant to evidence input order and irrelevant unprofiled blocks. Outside-profile closure is checked separately by requiring every claim outside $C$ to remain outside the authorization boundary. Irrelevant-evidence invariance does not apply to arbitrary modifications of a final evidence package. Every supplied block must still satisfy manifest lineage and attestation requirements, even when it is irrelevant to $Q(c)$. These are formalized properties tested over generated packages, not an exhaustive proof over arbitrary profiles and not statements about biological truth.

Release artifacts record protocol hashes, profile hashes, source revisions, exact public asset identifiers, and official data hashes. A release is rejected when a required artifact is absent, any source revision ends in \texttt{-dirty}, an expected benchmark decision changes, or a broad prohibited claim becomes true. The release gate therefore checks reproducibility and scope together. It cannot guarantee journal acceptance, profile consensus, or human usefulness of an explanation.
