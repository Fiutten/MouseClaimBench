\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This paper presented MouseClaimBench, an evidence-constrained \ac{kbs} for computational mouse-brain claims. The method externalizes a versioned knowledge profile, preserves original-scale evidence facts and their provenance, applies prioritized non-compensatory rules, and returns a machine-readable proof trace. Prediction, computational reproducibility, internal reproduction, external replication, topology, direction, structure--function association, mechanism, causality, and digital-twin scope remain separate knowledge targets.

The evaluation provides evidence at three levels. Exhaustive state tests establish deterministic rule behaviour for the mechanistic claim. A known-truth structural-equation benchmark reduces the false-positive rate from $0.271$ for a prediction shortcut to $0.019$, with a corresponding increase in false-negative rate from $0.007$ to $0.070$. Four real mouse-brain cases produce forty bounded decisions. The new engine reproduces all forty and attaches a complete trace to each one. SciFact and Tuebingen further show that retrieval and association cannot safely substitute for support and causal direction.

The result remains deliberately limited. Conformance to a knowledge profile is not biological truth. Only the computational mouse-brain profile is evaluated, the \ac{microns} result is internal to one volume, and none of the cases supports external replication, causal whole-brain identification, or a complete digital twin. The system is not a new simulator, fact checker, causal-discovery method, or automated reviewer.

Future work should first strengthen the current domain rather than pursue superficial breadth. The mouse profile should be frozen before evaluation on independently selected studies. Local structure--function inference should be tested across animals or resources and with network-dependent variance estimators. Upstream scientific verifiers and causal methods can be integrated as provenance-bearing fact producers. Semantic interoperability can then be extended through PROV-O-compatible export, profile-difference analysis, and explicit defeaters. A second domain profile is justified only when its vocabulary, predicates, and validation cases are independently available.

The main conclusion is precise. Accurate prediction and reproducible execution do not by themselves authorize mechanistic, causal, or digital-twin wording. MouseClaimBench represents that distinction as inspectable knowledge, executes it without compensation, and records why every bounded conclusion was reached.
