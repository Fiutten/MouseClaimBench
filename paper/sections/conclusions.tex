\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusions}

MouseClaimBench provides an integrity-aware knowledge-based method for authorizing bounded scientific claims from computational mouse-brain evidence. Its authorization contracts jointly bind graph conformance, package integrity, domain evidence semantics, and category-complete non-compensatory deficits. Authorization requires structural conformance, every mandatory domain block to be admissibly passed, and an integrity-clean package. The result is tied to a named profile and includes every violated deficit category, an actionable witness for each category, artifact identities, and protocol revisions.

The revised evaluation separates acquisition, specification verification, controlled threats, and empirical application. The acquisition ledger covers all 60 claim--evidence relations and 22 predicate contracts without claiming external consensus. Python and the separately implemented \ac{asp} path agree on domain decisions and deficits across all 5,677 generated cases. External \ac{shacl} independently recovers the expected structural decisions and structural deficits, including the canonical $S=1$, $A=0$ case. The executable final gate reproduces all eight states of $S\land A\land I$. A one-edge perturbation evaluates 221 profiles and 3,094 fixed artifact decisions. Counterfactual repair and minimality checks show no violation across 9,839 repaired packages and 51,480 individual deficits. These results establish implementation conformance and explanation fidelity relative to profile v2. They do not validate the scientific completeness of the policy.

The integrity results cover 2,550 attacked packages spanning all 255 non-empty compositions of eight original attack families across each of 10 claims, with exact invariant traces and no false authorization. A separate 100-package suite verifies nine additional reference and attestation regressions. Six direct API regressions add duplicate-identifier, reflexive-relation, and composite data-generation identity cases. The 20 coherent-forgery controls all remain authorized, which exposes rather than conceals the unresolved need for external trust anchors. The scientific applications preserve the same boundaries. One prospective DANDI protocol remains negative because its frozen subject requirement is unavailable. A second authorizes only population-response prediction across 32 mice. Post-outcome threshold sensitivity exposes both decisions without providing criterion validity. Dependence-aware \ac{microns} inference confirms one local observational structure--function association in two pre-fixed hold-outs. No evaluated case authorizes a causal mechanism, external biological replication, whole-brain coverage, or a complete entity-specific mouse-brain digital twin.

The next scientific requirement is independent assessment of the 92 v2 claim, predicate, and relation items by real domain experts, followed by evaluation of category-complete traces with human users. Independently proposed profiles, mandatory-to-review substitutions, and externally justified thresholds are needed for stronger sensitivity and construct-validity evidence. The integrity model should add signed attestations, archive trust policies, and adaptive attacks, while future external endpoints should be registered across laboratories and animals. The immediate conclusion remains narrower and testable: predictive accuracy, reproducible execution, and valid graph structure cannot compensate for missing scientific evidence or incoherent provenance under the declared profile.
