\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evaluation examines MouseClaimBench as a knowledge representation and inference system. It does not compare neural architectures and it does not claim state-of-the-art performance on the external datasets. The experiments separate four questions that can otherwise be confused. The first concerns logical coverage of the rule base. The second concerns claim errors when structural truth is known independently. The third concerns faithful inference over real mouse-brain artifacts. The fourth concerns behaviour under external failure modes and reproducible release constraints.

\subsection{Experimental protocol}
\label{subsec:experimental-protocol}

Table~\ref{tab:experiment-design} defines the evaluation questions, evidence units, and strongest interpretation allowed by each result. The final column is part of the protocol. A complete inference trace is evidence of explainability and conformance, not evidence that the underlying biological predicate is true. A synthetic oracle result evaluates the policy under its generator, not all possible scientific domains.

\input{tables/experiment_design}

Logical evaluation begins at profile load time. The parser must reject duplicate claims, repeated evidence requirements, duplicate rule identifiers, ambiguous priorities, incomplete conclusion coverage, and a missing all-passed terminal rule. The mechanistic claim has four required blocks and each block can take five evidence states. The test suite evaluates all $5^{4}=625$ assignments. Expected conclusions are computed from the declared precedence $F\succ R\succ U\succ N\succ P$. Separate tests cover undeclared claims, missing facts, fact-index consistency, deterministic graph export, and equivalence between the legacy interface and the new knowledge engine.

The primary known-truth experiment contains five \ac{sem} regimes. They represent independence, a common cause, a direct $X\rightarrow Y$ relation, the reverse $Y\rightarrow X$ relation, and a direct relation with an explicit intervention. Each regime generates 100 independent cases. Every case contains three cohorts of 600 observations for fitting, held-out evaluation, and internal reproduction. The data-generating graph and intervention availability define the reference claim set before any evidence rule is evaluated. MouseClaimBench receives finite-sample diagnostics for prediction, topology specificity, direction, and intervention rather than the oracle graph.

The 500 cases create 5,000 claim decisions for each policy. The knowledge policy is compared with a prediction shortcut that promotes successful prediction to topology, direction, mechanism, and causality. \ac{fpr} and \ac{fnr} are reported over claim decisions. Their Wilson intervals are descriptive because the ten decisions within one case are dependent. The confirmatory comparison uses the generated case as the unit. Within-case error counts are compared by an exact two-sided sign test over non-tied cases.

Four artifact-grounded cases cover complementary mouse-brain evidence. Allen \ac{vbn} examines internal reproduction, topology controls, and directional identifiability. Static Sensorium examines held-out response prediction and topographic specificity across five datasets. Dynamic Sensorium examines temporal prediction across two stored five-mouse cohorts. The local \ac{microns} case examines an observational structure--function endpoint in one cortical volume. Each adapter retains source observations and executes a source-specific predicate. The cases are not pooled into a common score.

The knowledge-system conformance audit loads the profile from its packaged \ac{yaml}, rebuilds the four case fact bases, and runs all ten claims for each case. Each result is compared field by field with the previously frozen decision, including claim, state, required blocks, block states, and rationale. The audit also requires a fired rule, a non-empty rule sequence, a profile hash, and one preserved fact for every required block. The typed graph schema must remain stable across cases while case statuses and sources are allowed to differ.

Two public datasets provide external controls. SciFact contains 809 training claims and 300 development claims with support, contradiction, and insufficient-information labels \citep{wadden2020scifact}. A \ac{bm} retriever, transparent overlap rule, and regularised logistic baseline examine whether retrieval can be confused with support. Tuebingen contains 108 cause--effect pairs \citep{mooij2016cause}. Transparent additive-noise, information-geometric, and linear non-Gaussian proxies examine whether association can be confused with causal direction. These are failure-mode controls rather than competitive task systems.

The legacy v2 suite is retained as regression evidence. It contains 144 constructed cases, 1,152 claim opportunities, 243 threshold combinations, and deterministic local perturbations. Its labels encode the implementation's own historical semantics. It can expose software regressions and effects of ablation, but it cannot independently validate the new profile. All release inputs are stored as \ac{json} artifacts with clean Git revisions.

\subsection{Rule behaviour, oracle performance, and real mouse cases}
\label{subsec:core-results}

The profile and engine pass the structural tests described above. Every one of the 625 mechanistic assignments reaches exactly one expected conclusion under the declared priority. A failure blocks the claim even when another block requires review or is missing. Review is selected when no failure exists. Unknown and not-applicable remain distinguishable. The all-passed assignment is the only route to support. These tests establish total and deterministic policy execution for the tested claim. They do not establish that the scientific taxonomy is complete.

The oracle benchmark produces 1,954 \acp{tp}, 56 \acp{fp}, 2,844 \acp{tn}, and 146 \acp{fn}. The corresponding \ac{fpr} is $0.0193$ with a descriptive Wilson interval of $[0.0149,0.0250]$. The \ac{fnr} is $0.0695$ with an interval of $[0.0594,0.0812]$. The prediction shortcut produces 2,085 \acp{tp}, 785 \acp{fp}, 2,115 \acp{tn}, and 15 \acp{fn}. Its \ac{fpr} is $0.2707$ and its \ac{fnr} is $0.0071$. The non-compensatory policy therefore exchanges some sensitivity for 729 fewer false authorizations under the declared regimes.

The case-level comparison provides the inferential result. MouseClaimBench has fewer claim errors in 263 of the 500 generated cases. The shortcut has fewer errors in 77 cases and 160 cases are tied. Among 340 non-tied cases, the exact two-sided sign-test value is $p=6.41\times10^{-25}$. This result supports the policy for these five generated regimes. It does not establish optimality under other generators or biological neural dynamics. Fig.~\ref{fig:oracle-benchmark} and Table~\ref{tab:oracle-policy-comparison} report both false authorization and false rejection so that increased conservativeness remains visible.

The real-case inference produces 10 supported, 7 blocked, 7 uncertain, and 16 out-of-scope conclusions. No case fires the external-review rule because none of the current adapters emits that evidence state. Controlled unit tests cover that branch. No real case supports external replication, causality, or a complete digital twin. Fig.~\ref{fig:real-case-matrix} displays the full forty-cell matrix.

\input{figures/claimbench_real_case_matrix}

Allen provides strong internal reproduction but remains a negative mechanistic case. Median cross-mouse and split-half correlations are $0.891$ and $0.990$. Nevertheless, Allen topology performs $-0.0054$ below the median permutation, the paired-advantage bootstrap lower bound is $-0.0075$, and only $0.21$ of declared permutations are outperformed. All six latency and lead--lag criteria equal zero. The knowledge engine retains computational reproducibility and internal reproduction, while the failed topology and direction facts block mechanism.

Static Sensorium supports prediction and topology within its resource. Across five datasets, the median best predictive correlation is $0.341$. Median gains over mean and scrambled baselines are $0.095$ and $0.068$. The topographic control passes in all five datasets, with a median observed Spearman coefficient of $0.177$ and median effect over its null of $0.178$. Dynamic Sensorium supports temporal prediction and internal reproduction. The temporal \ac{svd} baseline exceeds mean response in four of five and five of five mice under the two stored comparisons, with median gains of $0.039$ and $0.031$. Neither case provides intervention, external replication, or biological directional identification.

The \ac{microns} conclusion is local and observational. The all-pairs readout-location endpoint was fixed after discovery and evaluated in two non-overlapping hold-out windows. Discovery contains 1,000 units and 2,095 connected directed pairs. The hold-outs contain 992 and 999 units with 1,926 and 1,922 connected pairs. Distance-matched deltas are $0.0215$, $0.0210$, and $0.0179$, with unit-cluster weighted bootstrap intervals $[0.0166,0.0263]$, $[0.0142,0.0263]$, and $[0.0115,0.0238]$. Degree-matched deltas are $0.0403$, $0.0380$, and $0.0286$, with positive lower bounds in all windows. The supported statement is a bounded local structure--function association. It is not causal, externally replicated, or whole-brain.

The conformance audit obtains exact agreement for all 40 decisions and complete traces for all 40. Ten inferences fire the all-passed rule, seven fire the failure veto, seven fire the missing-evidence rule, and sixteen fire the scope-boundary rule. The exported graph contains 36 nodes and 45 edges. Its node inventory comprises one profile, ten claims, ten evidence blocks, five evidence states, five rules, and five decisions. The graph has twenty \emph{requires} relations, fifteen profile declarations, five status tests, and five conclusion relations. This result demonstrates faithful migration from the frozen policy to an explicit knowledge architecture. It does not independently validate the biological predicates.

\subsection{External controls, ablations, and release status}
\label{subsec:external-release-results}

SciFact separates evidence retrieval from support inference. Retrieval recall@5 is $0.899$ over 188 claims with annotated evidence, while the gold document is missed for 19 claims. The cited-text shortcut produces 35 false authorizations and 60 false rejections. Its \ac{fpr} is $0.199$ and its \ac{fnr} is $0.484$. A regularised logistic baseline is trained on 809 training claims, with a support threshold of $0.566$ selected under an \ac{fpr} constraint of $0.10$. On the untouched development split it reduces \ac{fpr} to $0.068$, but \ac{fnr} rises to $0.718$. Lower false authorization can therefore produce severe loss of supportable claims.

The Tuebingen adapter attempts direction for 103 of 108 pairs. Accuracy is $0.485$, with a Wilson interval of $[0.391,0.581]$. Weighted accuracy is $0.544$, with a pair-bootstrap interval of $[0.415,0.665]$. Agreement among all three proxies reaches $0.750$ accuracy but covers only $3.7\%$ of pairs. Correlation alone would promote a directional statement in 79 pairs under the operational threshold. This negative control shows why an association fact cannot satisfy the causal-direction requirement.

The legacy conformance suite still has engineering value. The full v2 implementation reproduces all 1,152 expected binary decisions. Removing topology or direction introduces 12 false authorizations in each ablation. A compensatory weighted score produces 84 false authorizations and 75 false rejections. The threshold grid contains 108 cells that meet the declared conformance criterion and 135 that do not. Local one-field perturbations produce 503 supported, 622 blocked, and 27 uncertain decisions. Figs.~\ref{fig:ablation} and \ref{fig:sensitivity} present these regression results. They show that the components are non-redundant under the constructed contract cases, but they are not empirical threshold calibration.

The KBS release gate evaluates nine required artifact families, including an audit of the current manuscript wording. Every artifact is present, every expected decision is reproduced, and no recorded revision ends in `-dirty'. The gate verifies the profile identifier, version, SHA-256 digest, forty decision matches, and forty complete traces. Its final state is \path{knowledge_system_method_package_ready_with_declared_boundaries}. The passing state concerns method and artifact consistency. It does not remove the four active boundaries: cross-domain empirical generality, external biological replication, improved human outcomes, and complete causal digital-twin scope.

\input{tables/release_summary}

Taken together, the experiments support a bounded knowledge-system conclusion. The rule base is total for the tested assignments, reduces false authorization relative to a predictive shortcut under known structural regimes, preserves forty frozen mouse-case conclusions, provides complete inference traces, and exposes two external substitution failures. The evidence does not establish universal scientific validity, state-of-the-art task performance, causal neuroscience, or a complete digital mouse brain.
