\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evaluation tests ClaimBench as a decision-support mechanism. It does not compare predictive architectures or claim state-of-the-art performance on the external datasets. The object of evaluation is the decision boundary between available evidence and admissible scientific wording. Protocol and results are presented together so that each numerical outcome remains attached to the question it answers.

\subsection{Evaluation design and evidence sources}
\label{subsec:evaluation-design}

The experimental design contains seven questions, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:experiment-design}. The first two concern internal validity of the decision rule. The next two use public external datasets to test evidence-retrieval and causal-direction failure modes. The fifth evaluates claim discovery and \ac{llm} authority. The final two examine component necessity and reproducible release integrity. Every experiment has a stored \ac{json} artifact, an expected decision, and a bounded interpretation.

\input{tables/experiment_design}

The synthetic benchmark contains 144 cases from 12 scenario families. Each case is evaluated against eight claim opportunities, producing 1,152 binary claim decisions per evaluator. The families include prediction under common drive, leaderboard overstatement, reliability without structure, topology without direction, direction without topology, confounded and matched structure--function effects, mechanistic positive controls, causal local controls, and incomplete digital-twin decoys. Deterministic variants perturb the numerical evidence while retaining the scenario semantics. The labels encode the operational contract and are therefore suitable for software verification, not for establishing independent scientific truth.

Threshold sensitivity evaluates $3^5=243$ combinations around the five nominal numerical thresholds. A cell is classified as safe when it produces no false authorizations and \ac{cni} does not exceed $0.25$ over the synthetic suite. A cell is dangerous when at least one unsupported claim is authorized. The uncertainty experiment separately evaluates the nominal case plus positive and negative perturbations of $0.03$ for each numerical evidence field, following Equation~\ref{eq:claim-stability}.

SciFact contributes 300 claims from its development split, including 124 support, 64 contradiction, and 112 not-enough-information labels \citep{wadden2020scifact}. A local \ac{bm} retriever returns five candidate abstracts and a transparent token-overlap rule estimates rationale support. This is intentionally not a competitive verification model. Tuebingen contributes 108 cause-effect pairs and compares transparent additive-noise, information-geometric, and linear non-Gaussian proxies \citep{mooij2016cause}. The causal experiment records direction accuracy, coverage, consensus, and how often correlation alone would have authorized a directional statement.

All numerical values reported below come from the frozen benchmark artifacts generated at revision \texttt{4fecaaf}. The manuscript does not manually edit those artifacts. This distinction matters for the text audit. The reported 120 candidates refer to a revision-specific mouse-brain modelling project corpus that was audited. They do not imply that the final submitted manuscript has already been independently reviewed.

\subsection{Known-truth gate, ablation, and sensitivity results}
\label{subsec:known-truth-results}

The full non-compensatory gate produces 528 true positive decisions and 624 true negative decisions across the 1,152 claim opportunities. No false positive or false negative is observed. This result verifies that the implementation reproduces the declared contract semantics on the constructed cases. It is not an independent validation of the thresholds because the known-truth labels use the same operational claim definitions.

The value of the gate becomes clearer when it is compared with the ablated evaluators in Table~\ref{tab:synthetic-evaluator-comparison}. Correlation-only and leaderboard-only evaluation authorize more than half of the unsupported claim opportunities, with \ac{ori} values of $0.521$ and $0.511$. Reliability-only evaluation reduces \ac{ori} to $0.285$ but blocks $38.3\%$ of supportable opportunities. A compensatory weighted score produces \ac{ori} $=0.135$ and \ac{cni} $=0.142$. Topology-only evaluation is conservative, but its \ac{cni} of $0.636$ shows why \ac{ori} cannot be interpreted alone.

\input{tables/synthetic_evaluator_comparison}

Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation} shows the \ac{ori} profile for the comparable known-truth evaluators. The topology and direction ablations each introduce 12 false authorizations, corresponding to \ac{ori} $=0.019$. The reproducibility ablation produces no error in this particular suite because the other blocks and scenario construction already prevent additional authorizations. It is therefore not classified as independent positive evidence for that component. This negative ablation result is retained rather than hidden.

\input{figures/claimbench_ablation}

The threshold grid identifies 108 safe cells and 135 dangerous cells. Thus, $44.4\%$ of the tested configurations satisfy the declared safe-cell criterion, while $55.6\%$ authorize at least one unsupported claim. The smallest non-zero \ac{ori} in the dangerous region is $0.0016$. This shows that the nominal behaviour is not a consequence of every nearby threshold choice. The decision rule has a non-trivial safe region, but the region must be reported and the thresholds cannot be described as universally robust.

The uncertainty experiment produces 503 supported, 622 blocked, and 27 uncertain decisions. No unsupported claim is marked as supported. Twenty-five supportable claims become uncertain under local perturbations. The remaining two uncertain cases are unsupported rather than falsely promoted. These results confirm that the third state preserves instability, although they do not provide statistical calibration. Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity} summarizes the safe and dangerous threshold regions together with the uncertainty outcome.

\subsection{External claim and causal controls}
\label{subsec:external-controls}

The SciFact retriever obtains recall@5 of $0.899$ over the 188 claims with annotated evidence. Nineteen claims with gold evidence are not retrieved in the first five positions. The lexical shortcut creates 35 false support decisions and 60 false negatives, yielding \ac{ori} $=0.199$ and \ac{cni} $=0.484$. The \ac{bm}-plus-rationale rule creates 44 false supports and 61 false negatives, with \ac{ori} $=0.250$ and \ac{cni} $=0.492$. A high retrieval recall therefore coexists with weak support authorization. This is the intended diagnostic result. Document retrieval, rationale overlap, and scientific support cannot be collapsed into one successful score.

The Tuebingen adapter attempts direction on 103 of 108 pairs. Aggregate accuracy is $0.485$ and weighted accuracy is $0.544$. Increasing the direction-margin threshold reduces coverage without producing a reliable monotonic gain in accuracy. Requiring all three proxies to agree leaves only $3.7\%$ coverage, although accuracy on that small subset reaches $0.75$. These values do not support a causal-discovery contribution. They support abstention and claim blocking because 79 pairs have absolute correlation above the operational threshold and would be overclaimed if association alone authorized direction.

\input{tables/external_controls}

The two controls answer different questions. SciFact shows that evidence retrieval can succeed while support classification remains unsafe. Tuebingen shows that strong dependence can coexist with weak directional identification. Neither dataset validates the complete ClaimBench taxonomy. Their role is to demonstrate that the principal failure modes also occur outside the handcrafted claim scenarios.

\subsection{Claim discovery, artifact audit, and release integrity}
\label{subsec:release-results}

The deterministic claim-discovery audit identifies 120 candidates in the frozen mouse-brain modelling source corpus. The distribution contains 43 mechanistic, 41 predictive, 10 causal, 8 digital-twin, 7 structure--function, 6 reproducibility, 3 generalization, and 2 state-of-the-art candidates. No external \ac{llm} \ac{api} is called, no optional \ac{llm} candidate file is loaded, and no candidate is authoritative. These counts measure extraction coverage on one project corpus. They do not measure precision, recall, or agreement with expert annotations.

The artifact-level manuscript audit evaluates seven declared contracts against the same frozen corpus and reports no active blocked-wording hits. Risk patterns related to digital twins, causality, and state-of-the-art language are present only in negated or boundary-setting contexts. This is a useful internal test of wording control, but it is not independent evidence. A blinded multi-author corpus is required before claiming general manuscript-auditing performance.

The reproduction manifest executes 15 stages and all 15 match their expected decisions. The unified report passes 10 of 10 criteria. The threat model passes 7 of 7 checks with no failed critical threat. The release gate finds no missing, dirty, or failing required artifacts at the frozen revision. Table~\ref{tab:release-summary} separates these engineering checks from the scientific interpretation they permit.

\input{tables/release_summary}

The release evidence establishes traceability and repeatable execution for the frozen package. It does not establish external replication, reviewer benefit, or general validity of the claim taxonomy. The combined experimental result is consequently narrower: ClaimBench implements a reproducible non-compensatory decision rule, exposes specific overclaiming failures in known-truth and external controls, and preserves explicit claim boundaries under the evaluated release.
