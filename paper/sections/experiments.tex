\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evaluation addresses the decision policy implemented by MouseClaimBench. It does not compare neural architectures and it does not claim state-of-the-art performance on the external datasets. The primary question is whether a non-compensatory contract authorizes fewer unsupported statements than a predictive shortcut when the reference claims are defined independently of the evidence thresholds. The remaining experiments test whether the same representation can preserve the narrower conclusions supported by real mouse-brain artifacts, expose two external failure modes, and produce a reproducible release with unresolved evidence left visible.

\subsection{Experimental protocol}
\label{subsec:experimental-protocol}

Table~\ref{tab:experiment-design} defines five evaluation questions, their evidence units, and the strongest interpretation permitted by each result. This last field is part of the protocol. It prevents a successful software check from being reported as biological validation or improved human decision making.

\input{tables/experiment_design}

The primary synthetic experiment contains five structural-equation regimes. They represent independence, a common cause, a direct $X\rightarrow Y$ relation, the reverse $Y\rightarrow X$ relation, and a direct relation with an explicit intervention. Each regime generates 100 independent cases. Every case contains three cohorts of 600 observations for fitting, held-out evaluation, and internal reproduction. The data-generating graph and intervention availability define the reference claim set before any evidence rule is evaluated. The decision policy receives only finite-sample diagnostics for prediction, topology specificity, direction, and intervention. Therefore, the oracle labels do not inherit the thresholds used by the contract.

The 500 cases create 5,000 claim decisions for each policy. MouseClaimBench v3 is compared with a prediction shortcut that promotes a successful predictive test to topology, direction, mechanism, and causality. False-positive and false-negative rates are reported over claim decisions. Their Wilson intervals are descriptive because the ten decisions within one generated case are dependent. The confirmatory policy comparison uses each generated case as the unit. The number of claim-decision errors is compared within a case, followed by an exact two-sided sign test over non-tied cases. This analysis avoids treating the ten correlated decisions as ten independent policy comparisons.

Four artifact-grounded cases cover complementary mouse-brain evidence. The Allen \ac{vbn} case examines internal reproduction, topology controls, and directional identifiability. Static Sensorium examines held-out response prediction and topographic specificity over five datasets. Dynamic Sensorium examines temporal prediction over five mice. The local \ac{microns} case examines an observational structure--function endpoint in one cortical volume. These sources are not pooled into a common numerical score. Each adapter retains the original observations and applies a source-specific predicate. The resulting matrix contains 40 case--claim dispositions. It is an evidence audit rather than a statistical sample.

Two public controls examine transfer beyond the mouse-brain artifacts. SciFact provides 809 training claims and 300 development claims, with 124 support, 64 contradiction, and 112 not-enough-information labels \citep{wadden2020scifact}. A \ac{bm} retriever, a transparent overlap rule, and a regularised logistic baseline test whether retrieval quality is sufficient for support authorization. Tuebingen provides 108 cause--effect pairs \citep{mooij2016cause}. Transparent additive-noise, information-geometric, and linear non-Gaussian proxies test whether association is sufficient for directional wording. These local baselines are deliberately auditable. They are controls for the contract logic and are not presented as competitive task systems.

The legacy v2 suite is retained as a regression test. It contains 144 constructed cases, 1,152 claim opportunities, 243 threshold combinations, and deterministic local perturbations. Its labels encode the same operational semantics as the implementation. It can verify conformance and reveal threshold dependence, but it cannot independently validate the taxonomy or its thresholds. All primary outputs are stored as \ac{json} artifacts with a clean Git revision. The expert-study package contains 50 unlabeled manuscript items and a preregistration-ready analysis plan. No participant has been recruited and no human-effect result is reported.

\subsection{Policy performance and artifact-grounded mouse cases}
\label{subsec:policy-and-mouse-results}

The oracle benchmark yields a clear but finite-sample result. MouseClaimBench produces 1,954 true positives, 56 false positives, 2,844 true negatives, and 146 false negatives. The corresponding \ac{fpr} is $0.0193$ with a descriptive Wilson interval of $[0.0149,0.0250]$. The \ac{fnr} is $0.0695$ with an interval of $[0.0594,0.0812]$. The prediction shortcut produces 2,085 true positives, 785 false positives, 2,115 true negatives, and 15 false negatives. Its \ac{fpr} is $0.2707$ and its \ac{fnr} is $0.0071$. The contract therefore exchanges some sensitivity for a reduction of 729 false authorizations under the declared regimes.

The case-level comparison is the relevant inferential result. MouseClaimBench has fewer claim errors in 263 of the 500 generated cases. The shortcut has fewer errors in 77 cases and 160 cases are tied. Among the 340 non-tied cases, the exact two-sided sign-test value is $p=6.41\times10^{-25}$. This result supports the non-compensatory policy for these five generated regimes. It does not establish optimality for other data-generating processes or for biological neural dynamics. Fig.~\ref{fig:oracle-benchmark} reports the error rates and the case-level comparison.

The real-case audit produces 10 supported, 7 blocked, 7 uncertain, and 16 out-of-scope dispositions. No real case supports external replication, causal mechanism, or a mouse digital twin. This inventory is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:real-case-matrix}. The pattern is more informative than a global pass rate because each row identifies which claim families are admissible for one source.

\input{figures/claimbench_real_case_matrix}

Allen provides a strong internal-reproduction result but a negative mechanistic-identifiability case. The median cross-mouse and split-half correlations are $0.891$ and $0.990$. Nevertheless, the Allen topology performs $-0.0054$ below the median permutation and the lower bound of the paired-advantage bootstrap is $-0.0075$. It outperforms only $0.21$ of the declared permutations. All six latency and lead--lag direction criteria equal zero. The admissible conclusion is therefore limited to computational reproducibility and internal reproduction. A mechanistic claim is blocked by the failed topology and direction blocks.

Static Sensorium supports a different statement. Across five datasets, the median best predictive correlation is $0.341$. The median gains over the mean and scrambled baselines are $0.095$ and $0.068$. The topographic control passes in all five datasets, with a median observed Spearman coefficient of $0.177$ and a median effect over its null of $0.178$. These observations support prediction, internal reproduction, and topology specificity within the resource. They provide no directed, interventional, or external-replication evidence. Dynamic Sensorium supports temporal prediction and internal reproduction. The temporal singular-value baseline exceeds the mean response in four of five and five of five mice under the two stored comparisons, with median paired gains of $0.039$ and $0.031$. Temporal predictability is not converted into biological direction.

The \ac{microns} endpoint is narrower and observational. The all-pairs readout-location endpoint was fixed after discovery and evaluated in two non-overlapping hold-out windows. Discovery contains 1,000 units and 2,095 connected directed pairs. The hold-outs contain 992 and 999 units with 1,926 and 1,922 connected pairs. Distance-matched deltas are $0.0215$, $0.0210$, and $0.0179$. Their unit-cluster weighted bootstrap intervals are $[0.0166,0.0263]$, $[0.0142,0.0263]$, and $[0.0115,0.0238]$. Degree-matched deltas are $0.0403$, $0.0380$, and $0.0286$, with positive lower bounds in all three windows. These values support a local structure--function association for the fixed descriptor. They do not identify causality, independent biological replication, or whole-brain scope. Table~\ref{tab:real-case-summary} records the observations that determine each boundary.

\subsection{External controls, conformance, and release status}
\label{subsec:external-and-release-results}

SciFact separates evidence retrieval from claim authorization. The retriever reaches recall@5 of $0.899$ over the 188 claims with annotated evidence, while failing to retrieve the gold document for 19 claims. The lexical shortcut produces 35 false authorizations and 60 false rejections. Its \ac{fpr} is $0.199$ and its \ac{fnr} is $0.484$. Adding the local rationale rule produces 44 false authorizations and 61 false rejections. A regularised logistic baseline is trained on the 809 training claims, with a support threshold of $0.566$ selected on that split under an \ac{fpr} constraint of $0.10$. On the untouched development split it reduces the \ac{fpr} to $0.068$, but its \ac{fnr} rises to $0.718$. The result exposes a real decision trade-off. High retrieval recall does not authorize scientific support, and lower false authorization can be purchased at unacceptable loss of supportable claims.

The Tuebingen adapter attempts direction for 103 of 108 pairs. Accuracy is $0.485$, with a Wilson interval of $[0.391,0.581]$. Weighted accuracy is $0.544$, with a pair-bootstrap interval of $[0.415,0.665]$ using the official pair weights. Raising the direction margin reduces coverage without a reliable improvement in accuracy. Agreement among all three proxies reaches $0.750$ accuracy but covers only $3.7\%$ of the pairs. Correlation alone would promote a directional statement in 79 pairs under the operational association threshold. The adapter therefore supplies a negative control for causal authorization rather than a causal-discovery result. Fig.~\ref{fig:external-controls} presents both external failure modes and Table~\ref{tab:external-controls} gives their numerical summary.

The legacy conformance suite still has engineering value. The full v2 implementation reproduces all 1,152 expected binary decisions. Removing topology or direction introduces 12 false authorizations in each case. A compensatory weighted score produces 84 false authorizations and 75 false rejections. The threshold grid contains 108 cells that meet the declared conformance criterion and 135 that do not. Local one-field perturbations produce 503 supported, 622 blocked, and 27 uncertain decisions. Figs.~\ref{fig:ablation} and \ref{fig:sensitivity} show these regression results. They demonstrate that nearby implementation choices matter, but they are not used to claim independent threshold calibration.

The v3 release gate finds every required artifact, no failing expected decision, and no artifact revision ending in `-dirty'. Its final state is \texttt{methodological\_package\_ready\_human\_effect\_unvalidated}. Three blockers remain active: improved human decision quality has not been measured, external biological replication is absent, and no case supports a complete or causal mouse-brain digital twin. Table~\ref{tab:release-summary} preserves these blockers beside the passing engineering checks. The human protocol defines a double-blinded crossover pilot with 24 participants, 50 manuscript items, participant and item random effects, and adjudicated reference labels. It is an executable study design, not a completed experiment.

Taken together, the experiments support a bounded methodological conclusion. MouseClaimBench reduces unsupported authorization relative to a predictive shortcut in a known-truth finite-sample benchmark. It also preserves the distinct conclusions supported by four heterogeneous mouse-brain artifacts and exposes retrieval and directional failure modes in two public controls. The experiments do not demonstrate improved reviewer decisions, universal threshold validity, causal neuroscience, or a complete digital mouse brain.
