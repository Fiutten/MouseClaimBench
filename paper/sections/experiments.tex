\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evaluation is organized into four evidence classes. Knowledge acquisition asks whether the author-defined policy is fully traceable. Specification verification asks whether the implementation follows that policy and whether its explanations are counterfactually faithful. Controlled threat evaluation asks which declared integrity violations are detected and which coherent forgeries remain outside the trust model. Empirical application asks whether bounded decisions are preserved on existing and pre-access frozen mouse-brain resources. Performance is reported separately and is not used as scientific validation. Table~\ref{tab:experiment-design-v2} defines the evidence unit and maximum interpretation for every question.

\subsection{Experimental design and decision rules}
\label{subsec:experimental-design}

The acquisition audit compares the executable profile with its machine-readable basis and bibliography. Acceptance requires exact coverage of 10 claim definitions, 22 evidence blocks and predicate contracts, and 60 claim--evidence relations. Every relation must have a unique identifier and claim-specific necessity rationale. Every predicate must state evaluation ownership, decision-rule scope, required observations, sources, and exceptions. All source identifiers must resolve to the bibliography, and the audit must retain false values for independent content validity and human validation. The historical 29-item v1 panel packet is excluded. A separate v2 protocol contains 92 prospective review items covering the 10 claims, 22 predicates, and 60 relations, but has zero completed independent raters and is not counted as evidence.

The contract benchmark generates five case families from every profile claim. They contain pristine complete packages, one changed status, one omitted mandatory block, one omitted required observation, and all pairwise combinations of the four non-passing states. For a claim with $q_c$ required blocks and $m_c$ mandatory observation fields across those blocks, the number of cases is
\begin{equation}
N_c=1+5q_c+m_c+16\binom{q_c}{2}.
\label{eq:contract-case-count}
\end{equation}
The terms represent one pristine package, four status mutations and one omission per block, one omission per observation field, and 16 status combinations per block pair. Table~\ref{tab:profile-v2-claims} reports every $N_c$, which sums to 5,497. Each case has an exact expected authorization and deficit set defined from Eqs.~\ref{eq:admissibility}--\ref{eq:core-authorization}. Python evaluates all cases and is compared with \ac{shacl} on the 5,497 structural contracts. The separately implemented \ac{asp} program is compared on 262 cases selected deterministically by mutation family. Endpoints are zero false authorizations, zero false rejections, exact deficit-set recovery, and exact agreement on each declared comparison set. JSON-LD round-trip equivalence is evaluated through graph isomorphism.

Property-based verification uses 1,000 deterministic random evidence packages for each of the 10 claims. Every package is evaluated for soundness, completeness, exact deficits, input-order invariance, irrelevant-evidence invariance, and monotonic degradation whenever a passed block exists. Outside-profile closure is evaluated separately. Property-based tests generate additional status and observation mutations during the software suite. The reported 55,031 checks refer to the frozen deterministic experiment, not to an exhaustive proof over arbitrary future profiles.

Structural sensitivity holds 14 artifact decisions fixed: the bounded target and strict complete-twin claim for each of five retrospective cases and two pre-access DANDI cases. It evaluates the baseline profile, all 60 profiles obtained by removing one required relation, and all 160 profiles obtained by adding one currently unrequired evidence block to one claim. For decision $d_j$ and variant $\Pi_k$, the flip indicator is
\begin{equation}
F_{jk}=\mathbb{1}\!\left[A_{\Pi_k}(d_j)\neq A_{\Pi}(d_j)\right].
\label{eq:profile-sensitivity-flip}
\end{equation}
Removing a relation is a weakening perturbation and can only expand authorization. Adding a relation is a conservative extension and can only contract it when the added block is not admissibly passed. The variants diagnose policy dependence. They are not proposed as scientifically correct alternatives and are not used to select or calibrate profile v2.

Counterfactual explanation fidelity is evaluated on another deterministic set of 1,000 packages per claim. Every non-authorized package is repaired by replacing exactly the reported deficits with complete passing attestations. The repaired package must authorize with no residual deficit. Each repaired block is then restored individually to its original defective state. Authorization must again fail with that block as the sole remaining deficit, implementing Eq.~\ref{eq:counterfactual-explanation}. Every witness must retain a non-empty block name, source, rule, and rationale. Finally, each of the 60 pristine claim--block relations is degraded independently. A single-reason baseline is evaluated descriptively by counting the simultaneous deficits it would omit. No claim about human usefulness is tested.

The original integrity benchmark applies eight attacks defined in Section~\ref{subsec:standards-integrity}. For each of the 10 claims, it evaluates one pristine package, every single attack, and all 28 pairwise attack combinations. This produces 370 packages, of which 360 are attacked. A false authorization occurs when an attacked package is authorized:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{FAR}_{\mathrm{attack}}=
\frac{\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{1}[m\text{ attacked}]A^{*}_{\Pi}(c_m,B_m,m)}
{\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{1}[m\text{ attacked}]}.
\label{eq:attack-far}
\end{equation}
We compare the complete integrity gate with the core profile alone, a hash-only baseline, and eight leave-one-control-out systems. Exact trace recovery requires the detected deficit codes to equal the applied attack set. A new compositional stress test then evaluates all $2^8-1=255$ non-empty attack subsets for each claim, producing 2,550 attacked packages. Two further negative controls per claim replace content and its declared hash coherently or forge internally consistent independence metadata. Because these controls contain no contradiction against an external truth anchor, authorization is the expected result. They expose the need for trusted archives, signatures, or external identity evidence rather than being counted as failures under the declared eight-invariant model. Neither benchmark is an adaptive security guarantee.

Scalability is measured on one Apple arm64 host with Python 3.14.4. Pristine authorization batches contain 100, 1,000, and 10,000 packages drawn cyclically from all profile claims. Each batch is repeated five times after 100 warm-up decisions. A separate experiment expands one valid digital-twin package to 25, 100, 1,000, and 5,000 uniquely content-addressed artifacts. Median wall-clock time and all repetitions are stored. These measurements characterize one implementation and host. No portable latency guarantee is inferred.

Three artifact-evaluation components complement the controlled benchmarks. The first reuses five result packages assembled during the preceding MouseBrainBench study and asks a different question: whether profile v2 authorizes their bounded target claim and strict digital-twin claim. These mappings are retrospective and are not counted as prospective validation. The second applies dependence-aware inference to \ac{microns}. Its endpoint was fixed after discovery and evaluated in two non-overlapping hold-out windows within one tissue volume. Directed pair regression controls log soma distance, its square, pre- and postsynaptic degree, and coarse cell type. Dependence is addressed with directed dyadic cluster covariance and 1,000 simultaneous node-label Freedman--Lane permutations. These hold-outs provide internal reproduction, not independent biological replication. The third component evaluates the two external \ac{dandi} protocols described below.

Two external \ac{dandi} protocols were committed before numerical asset access. The availability protocol was frozen in commit \texttt{b59a497} and the predictive protocol in \texttt{d039e66}. Their SHA-256 identities are retained in the result artifact, and the numerical result was committed later. This Git history is temporal evidence for the declared ordering, not third-party preregistration. DANDI:001176 was selected from metadata describing 29 subjects and simultaneous cholinergic axon, cortical \ac{ach}, pupil, and treadmill signals \citep{neyhart2024ach}. The protocol required at least 20 usable subjects and prohibited threshold or endpoint repair. After catalog inspection, 28 schema-valid sessions came from only 5 subjects. The predictive model was therefore not executed.

DANDI:000039 contains contrast-tuning recordings associated with a published visual-cortex study \citep{millman2020vip}. Before inspecting its asset catalog or values, the protocol fixed one lexicographically first \texttt{behavior+ophys.nwb} asset per subject, a trial-level population mean event response, five contrast and direction regressors, Ridge regression with $\alpha=1$, and an intercept comparator. The first 60\% of trials train the model, the next 20\% remain reserved, and the final 20\% form the test partition. Authorization requires at least 25 subjects, median held-out correlation at least 0.10, a positive 95\% mouse-bootstrap lower bound, at least 65\% positive subjects, and pooled squared error below the intercept. All conditions must pass. The bootstrap resamples subjects 5,000 times and therefore targets population stability across mice rather than trial-level independence.

\subsection{Knowledge traceability, structural sensitivity, and explanation fidelity}
\label{subsec:construct-validity-results}

The acquisition audit exactly covers the 10 claims, 22 evidence blocks, 22 predicate contracts, 60 claim--evidence relations, and 124 required observation slots. All 60 relation identifiers and necessity rationales are unique, and all 20 source identifiers resolve to the bibliography. The ledger separates upstream predicate execution from authorization checks and marks every relation as a source-grounded author requirement without independent consensus. This satisfies traceability of the current policy. It does not establish that the policy is complete or optimal.

The structural analysis evaluates 221 profiles and 3,094 profile--case combinations. The baseline authorizes four of the 14 fixed decisions. Among the 60 weakening variants, only removal of the \texttt{data\_quality} requirement from bounded prediction changes an authorization. It converts the Dynamic Sensorium target from a documented quality refusal into an authorization. This isolates the requirement that is decisive for that case rather than showing that it should be removed. Among 160 conservative extensions, 52 variants change at least one decision and produce 71 contractions. These contractions occur when an otherwise authorized case has no admissible attestation for the added block. Ninety-six variants change at least one deficit set even when authorization does not change.

\input{tables/profile_sensitivity_v2}

Counterfactual fidelity is evaluated on 10,000 packages. Of the 9,839 non-authorized packages, repairing every reported deficit is sufficient for authorization with no residual deficit. Restoring each deficit individually produces 51,480 minimality checks, all of which refuse authorization with the exact retained deficit. The same 51,480 deficit records retain non-empty witnesses, and all 60 pristine single-block degradations return the expected sole deficit. There are 8,632 packages with simultaneous deficits. A single-reason trace would omit 41,641 deficits across these packages. This establishes information retention and profile-relative counterfactual fidelity, not improved human decisions.

\subsection{Conformance and property-based verification results}
\label{subsec:conformance-results}

Table~\ref{tab:standards-formal-results} reports the principal conformance results. The Python engine produces no false authorization or false rejection in 5,497 cases and recovers every expected deficit set. The \ac{shacl} engine classifies the same 5,497 structural cases correctly and returns every expected structural deficit. The independent \ac{asp} implementation matches Python status and deficits in all 262 selected cases. The exported profile contains 318 \ac{rdf} triples representing all 10 claims and 22 evidence blocks. JSON-LD serialization and parsing preserve graph isomorphism.

\input{tables/standards_formal_results}

These results separate two conclusions. First, the full Python semantics agree with \ac{shacl} on the structural comparison and with \ac{asp} on the selected semantic subset. The study does not report three-way equivalence over all cases. Second, standard \ac{shacl} validation reproduces the structural contract. Structural completeness and deficit reporting are therefore not unique capabilities of MouseClaimBench. The additional contribution must be assessed through domain requirements, package integrity, controlled attacks, and real applications.

The 10,000 deterministic random packages produce 55,031 property checks with no violation. Soundness, completeness, deficit identity, input-order invariance, and irrelevant-evidence invariance each pass 10,000 checks. Monotonic degradation passes all 5,030 applicable checks. Outside-profile closure also passes. These results establish the operational semantics for profile v2. They do not prove that its evidence requirements are scientifically complete.

\subsection{Integrity attacks, ablation, and scalability}
\label{subsec:integrity-results}

Under the declared deterministic threat model, the complete gate detects the exact attack set in all 370 packages. It produces no false rejection on the 10 pristine packages and no false authorization on the 360 attacked packages. The core profile does not inspect package integrity and therefore authorizes all 360 attacked packages. Hash checking alone blocks content tampering but authorizes 280 packages affected by profile substitution, broken or cyclic lineage, false independence, cohort overlap, contradictory attestations, or missing block provenance.

\input{figures/integrity_ablation_v2_figure}

The leave-one-out result is uniform and informative. Removing any one of the eight controls creates 10 false authorizations, corresponding to the single-attack package for that omitted control in each claim. Pairwise attacks containing another enabled control remain blocked. Table~\ref{tab:integrity-ablation-v2} gives the exact values, while Fig.~\ref{fig:integrity-ablation-v2} shows the contrast between incomplete baselines and the full system. The result demonstrates necessity under the declared attack construction. It does not imply that the eight controls are sufficient for every real provenance threat.

\input{tables/integrity_ablation_v2}

The compositional stress test evaluates 2,560 packages, including one pristine package and all 255 non-empty attack subsets for each claim. The complete gate produces no false authorization in the 2,550 attacked packages and returns the exact combined deficit set in all 2,560 cases. This removes the original restriction to single and pairwise compositions but does not add new protected invariants. The trust-boundary controls provide the necessary negative result. All 20 internally coherent content or independence forgeries remain authorized with no integrity deficit because the package contains no externally verifiable contradiction. The system therefore detects inconsistency with its declared invariants. It does not authenticate the truth of self-consistent source metadata.

Batch throughput remains between 63,504 and 65,465 authorizations per second. Median time is 0.00153 s for 100 packages, 0.01575 s for 1,000, and 0.15419 s for 10,000. A valid package with 5,000 artifacts is evaluated in a median of 0.00255 s. These measurements describe the observed range on one host and are not an asymptotic complexity result. All pristine decisions remain authorized at every scale.

\subsection{Mouse-brain artifacts and prospective external applications}
\label{subsec:application-results}

The retrospective profile-v2 mapping contains five target claims and the corresponding five strict digital-twin claims. Three bounded target claims are authorized. Static Sensorium authorizes prediction for its declared five-dataset comparison. The \ac{ibl} case authorizes topology-specific behavioural prediction under its locked comparison. \ac{microns} authorizes one local observational structure--function association. Allen directed topology remains unauthorized because prediction, topology, direction, uncertainty, robustness, and data-quality requirements do not all pass. Dynamic Sensorium remains unauthorized because the stored response-reliability quality block requires review. None of the five cases authorizes the strict digital-twin claim.

The dependence-aware \ac{microns} analysis changes the status of the local association without changing its scope. The connected-pair coefficient is 0.0106 in discovery, 0.0178 in hold-out 1, and 0.0117 in hold-out 2. Directed dyadic standard errors are 0.00200, 0.00209, and 0.00205. Two-sided dyadic $p$-values are $1.39\times10^{-7}$, $5.78\times10^{-17}$, and $1.76\times10^{-8}$. The simultaneous node-label permutation $p$-value is 0.000999 in every window. Shared-neuron dependence is therefore addressed more conservatively than naive pair-level inference. All windows nevertheless come from one \ac{microns} volume, and readout-location similarity is a derived functional descriptor rather than a causal measurement.

The two prospective DANDI outcomes are deliberately asymmetric. DANDI:001176 fails the frozen availability condition with 5 usable subjects against a minimum of 20. No predictive fit is reported, and the endpoint is not weakened after seeing the catalog. DANDI:000039 yields 32 selected and usable mice. Its median held-out correlation is 0.310, the mouse-bootstrap 95\% interval is $[0.207,0.369]$, and 93.75\% of subjects have positive correlation. Pooled model squared error is 11.579 compared with 12.244 for the training-intercept baseline. All five frozen acceptance conditions pass, so the system authorizes only bounded population-response prediction within this resource.
