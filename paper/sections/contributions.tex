\section{Research Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

MouseClaimBench transforms the interpretation of computational mouse-brain results into a claim-level decision problem. The system does not assign a global quality score to a paper. It determines which evidence requirements pass, which fail, which were not observed, and which were not targeted by the protocol. The resulting artifact recommends an editorial disposition while preserving human authority. The paper makes five contributions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A domain-aware evidence contract.} The contract records original-scale observations, executable predicates, source revisions, required blocks, and admissible wording. It separates computational reproducibility, internal reproduction, and external replication rather than treating them as one property.

    \item \textbf{A five-disposition non-compensatory policy.} A failed required block vetoes the dependent claim. Missing evidence produces uncertainty, an untargeted block produces an out-of-scope decision, and non-automatable criteria are directed to external review. These workflow dispositions are distinct from local numerical stability.

    \item \textbf{An independently labelled structural-equation benchmark.} Five declared data-generating regimes produce 500 finite-sample cases. Their graph and intervention labels are independent of the thresholds used to evaluate the observations. The benchmark reports standard \ac{fpr}, \ac{fnr}, confidence intervals, and a paired policy comparison.

    \item \textbf{An artifact-grounded mouse-brain case matrix.} Allen \ac{vbn}, Static Sensorium, Dynamic Sensorium, and \ac{microns} are evaluated from stored observed fields without invented cross-domain normalization. The matrix exposes the strongest bounded claim supported by each resource and the evidence that blocks stronger wording.

    \item \textbf{A reproducible decision-support package with explicit unresolved validation.} SciFact and Tuebingen test external failure modes. The release gate checks clean artifact revisions and retains active blockers. A 50-item expert-study package is provided for ethics review and preregistration, but no human result is claimed because the study has not been executed.
\end{itemize}

These contributions support a bounded claim. MouseClaimBench implements and evaluates a traceable scientific-claim decision policy for the studied conditions. It does not establish universal claim validity, improved human review, causal-discovery performance, external biological replication, or a complete mouse-brain digital twin.
